## Tech & AI News

**Nvidia officially seals its $12.9B purchase of Hugging Face, buying its way into the open-source AI supply chain.** After weeks of reporting that it was in talks, Nvidia confirmed the deal on its own corporate blog, making Hugging Face — the repository more than 18 million developers use to share upwards of 3 million open models and 500,000 datasets — a subsidiary of the world's most valuable chipmaker. The strategic logic is straightforward: nearly everyone who downloads an open model from Hugging Face still needs somewhere to run it, and that's usually an Nvidia GPU, whether on-premise or in the cloud. It's Nvidia's second-largest acquisition on record, after the $20B Groq asset purchase in December, and it hands the company direct influence over the plumbing of the open-source AI ecosystem rather than just the chips underneath it. Analysts are still parsing the implications a week later — a September 15 Motley Fool piece argued the deal "changes everything" about how software-layer consolidation in AI will play out. Source: [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-to-acquire-hugging-face/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/nvidia-agrees-to-buy-hugging-face-for-almost-13-billion-ai-expansion.html)

**Google begins killing off Google Assistant, handing the keys to Gemini across Android and Wear OS.** The shutdown started rolling out September 4 and will phase out Assistant entirely from phones, tablets, Wear OS watches, headphones, and Android Auto-equipped cars, with no way to switch back once a device is migrated. It's the clearest signal yet that Google now treats Gemini not as a chatbot bolted onto search, but as the default interface layer for its entire consumer hardware ecosystem — capable of remembering where you put your passport, reading your screen, and acting across Google's apps rather than just answering questions. The rollout is expected to take several weeks to reach all users. Source: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-will-shut-down-google-assistant-starting-september-2026-as-gemini-takes-over-on-android-and-wear-os/)

**OpenAI's IPO slips to 2027, CFO tells staff, even as revenue keeps climbing.** Sarah Friar told employees in an internal update that OpenAI "will be a public company in 2027" — or sooner only "if our business continues to inflect" — walking back earlier Wall Street Journal reporting that the company was quietly prepping listing documents with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley for a possible September window. No filing has materialized. The delay comes despite strong underlying numbers: OpenAI's Q2 2026 revenue hit $6.7 billion, up 18% quarter-over-quarter from $5.7 billion in Q1. The company is instead spending September on product (ChatGPT's expanded Voice mode, deep research now built into Work and Codex) and on DevDay, its September 29 developer conference at Fort Mason in San Francisco. Source: [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/openai-said-it-would-list-in-september/)

**Nvidia's Jensen Huang tells Washington to stay out of AI safety.** In comments reported this week, Huang argued the industry doesn't need external AI regulation because companies like his are already incentivized to build safely — a notably more confident, less conciliatory stance than the "we welcome sensible regulation" line most frontier labs have taken publicly. Coming from the CEO whose chips now underpin nearly the entire AI buildout, and whose company just bought its way into the open-model ecosystem via Hugging Face, the remarks read as a marker in the ongoing fight over how much say governments should get in shaping frontier AI as the technology becomes more consequential and harder to inspect from outside. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/we-dont-need-ai-regulation-leave-safety-to-us-nvidias-jensen-huang-says/)

**Meta puts AI agents in charge of WhatsApp Business onboarding.** The company rolled out a feature letting AI agents handle the tedious setup work — catalog creation, message templates, account configuration — for the small and mid-sized businesses that run commerce through WhatsApp. It's a small-sounding change with a big install base behind it: WhatsApp Business already serves tens of millions of merchants, mostly outside the US, and any friction removed at onboarding compounds fast. It's also another data point in Meta's broader push to make "agentic commerce" — AI doing the operational work around a purchase, not just the chat — a default rather than a novelty. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/meta-now-lets-ai-agents-handle-the-boring-parts-of-whatsapp-business-setup/)

## Startup World

## Venture Capital

**CVC Capital Partners raises $10B for its largest-ever secondaries fund.** The vehicle, closed and reported September 3, is dedicated to buying and selling stakes in private companies on the secondary market — the mechanism that's become essential plumbing for an industry where startups are staying private for a decade or more and early investors and employees need some way to get liquidity before an IPO ever happens. A $10B pool of dry powder specifically for secondaries is itself a signal about how illiquid the private markets have become. Source: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/cvc-raises-10-billion-for-its-largest-ever-secondaries-fund)

**Accel closes $5B across its new Leaders Fund V and a sidecar vehicle**, aimed squarely at 20-25 concentrated bets averaging $200M each into what the firm calls the world's fastest-scaling AI companies. It's part of a broader pattern this year of firms moving away from spreading small checks across many startups and toward writing much larger checks into a shortlist of AI companies they're already convinced are winning — a barbell strategy that concentrates risk but also potential returns.

**The 2026 mega-fund cycle keeps compounding**: with Accel and CVC's raises added to the pile, live trackers now put total 2026 VC fundraising at roughly $81B across 76 funds, led by a16z's $15B vehicle and Sequoia's $7B target. A January Forbes analysis noted a16z's fund alone had "vacuumed up a fifth of venture dollars raised" the prior year — a concentration of capital in a handful of firms that's reshaping which startups get funded and on what terms. Source: [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/iainmartin/2026/01/09/a16zs-15-billion-fund-vacuumed-up-a-fifth-of-venture-dollars-raised-last-year/)

## Founder Biography: An Wang

Long before "immigrant founder builds a tech giant from nothing" became a Silicon Valley cliché, An Wang lived it in Boston. Born in Shanghai in 1920, the son of an English teacher, Wang showed an early gift for mathematics — entering the elite Shanghai Provincial High School at 13 and graduating from Chiao Tung University with an engineering degree in 1940, into a China at war. He came to the United States for graduate study, earning a master's and then a PhD in applied physics from Harvard by 1948, and went to work at Harvard's Computation Laboratory, where he co-invented a method for magnetic core memory — a way of storing data on tiny magnetized rings that became the standard memory technology for computers throughout the 1950s and 60s.

Rather than stay in a lab, Wang founded Wang Laboratories in 1951 with $600 in savings, initially as its only employee. In 1956 he sold his core-memory patent to IBM for $400,000 — money that gave the young company real runway. Wang Laboratories spent the next two decades building calculators and then word-processing systems, and by the 1970s and early 1980s it was one of the most successful high-technology companies in America, with Wang himself holding roughly 40 patents and, by the mid-1980s, a personal fortune that made him a billionaire — one of the first self-made Chinese-American billionaires in the country's history.

The company's decline is as instructive as its rise. Wang handed the presidency to his son Frederick in 1986, a succession that struggled as IBM's PC platform came to dominate the office-computing market Wang Labs had helped pioneer. An Wang returned to try to steady the company in 1989, the year before his death, but it wasn't enough: Wang Laboratories filed for bankruptcy in 1992 and was eventually absorbed by Dutch firm Getronics in 1999. What survives is less the company than the template — a technically brilliant immigrant founder, a fortune built on a single foundational patent, and a cautionary lesson about how hard it is to hand a fast-moving tech company to the next generation at exactly the moment the market shifts under it.
