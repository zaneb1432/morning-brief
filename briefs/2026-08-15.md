## TECH & AI NEWS

**Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash, undercutting itself on price just three weeks after the last model.** Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash on August 13, pitching it as the company's "most intelligent workhorse model yet" for coding, knowledge work, and autonomous agents — arriving barely three weeks after Gemini 3.6 Flash. The gains are concentrated in software engineering: the model jumped from 34.4% to 43.6% on the FrontierCode 1.1 Main benchmark and from roughly 49% to 65.3% on DeepSWE v1.1, alongside a 1,048,576-token context window and a 65,536-token output limit. Google also cut the introductory price roughly in half versus 3.6 Flash, to $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens, and says the model is better at recognizing when it's stuck, clarifying ambiguous instructions, and planning multi-step tool calls without hand-holding. The rapid-fire release cadence — three major Flash updates in under two months — signals Google is treating its cheap/fast tier as the main battleground against OpenAI and DeepSeek rather than reserving improvements for flagship Pro models, and underscores how "workhorse" models, not just frontier flagships, are now where the pricing and capability war is being fought. Source: [Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-3.7-flash/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-gemini-3-7-flash-targets-coding-and-agents-with-a-50-introductory-price-cut)

**DeepSeek fires back with V4 Pro, and this time it's raising prices, not slashing them.** DeepSeek formally released its official V4-Pro-0813 model on August 13, moving it into general availability across its API, app, and web platforms with significantly enhanced agent capabilities. Notably, the company priced V4 Pro well above its own V4 Flash model — roughly 9x the input price and 14x the output price by Reuters' reporting — a reversal of the price-war dynamics DeepSeek has been credited (or blamed) for triggering since 2025; independent price-tracking sites list somewhat lower absolute figures, so treat the exact multiple as directionally right but not fully reconciled. The launch is being watched closely because DeepSeek's cheaper V4 Flash had unexpectedly outperformed the April preview of V4 Pro on several independent benchmarks, so the "official" release is effectively DeepSeek proving it can still out-develop its own budget model. The move comes as the company steps up hiring and compute buildout, with Reuters reporting DeepSeek is pursuing a new funding round at a roughly $74 billion valuation just weeks after raising about $7.4 billion in its first outside financing round — a sign that even the industry's disruptive low-cost player is starting to charge premium prices for its best model as it competes for enterprise agent workloads against Gemini, GPT, and Claude. Source: [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/deepseek-releases-official-v4-pro-model-as-it-steps-up-expansion-4857986), [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/deepseek-releases-official-v4-pro-model-as-it-steps-up-expansion-ce7859d9de8bf622)

**xAI's Grok 4.6 muscles back onto the frontier — and undercuts rivals on price.** xAI launched Grok 4.6 on August 12, reusing the same 1.5-trillion-parameter foundation as Grok 4.5 but squeezing out gains through improved fine-tuning and reinforcement learning rather than a ground-up retrain. The model scored 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — up five points from Grok 4.5 and landing it roughly level with OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol, just behind Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 (63) and Claude Fable 5 (62) — while xAI held pricing flat at $2/$6 per million tokens, undercutting rivals at similar capability. Its agentic performance stood out in particular: a GDPval-AA v2 Elo of 1,753 (second only to Claude Opus 5) on real-world knowledge-work tasks, and an 88.4% score on Terminal-Bench v2.1, with the context window also expanding to 500,000 tokens for longer-running autonomous agent sessions. Coverage of the launch increasingly refers to the company as "SpaceXAI," reflecting xAI's closer integration with Musk's broader corporate structure, and the release puts xAI back in a three-way statistical tie for the frontier with OpenAI and Google, trailing only Anthropic on most independent benchmarks. Source: [xAI](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-6), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/grok-4-6-benchmarks-and-analysis)

**Anthropic starts invisibly watermarking everything Claude writes — and it's not just for Europe.** Anthropic confirmed it will embed machine-readable, invisible watermarks into text generated by Claude, with the policy covering models released on or after August 2, 2026, and a grace period for older systems running through December 2026. The immediate trigger is Article 50 of the EU AI Act, which became enforceable on August 2 and requires generative AI providers to mark synthetic content so platforms and regulators can identify it — but Anthropic is applying the watermark globally, not just to EU traffic, making it the first frontier lab to deploy production-scale text watermarking across its entire product line at once. The technique works by subtly biasing the token-selection process during generation rather than inserting hidden characters, so it doesn't change what the text says or looks like to a human reader; Anthropic says light editing likely won't remove it, but a full rewrite will, and coverage spans the Claude Platform API, claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, and Claude access through AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Foundry. The move puts pressure on OpenAI, Google, and Meta — all signatories to the same EU Code of Practice — to detail their own watermarking rollouts, and it's likely to reshape how platforms and publishers try to detect AI-generated text at scale. Source: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-says-it-will-watermark-text-generated-by-its-ai-models/)

**OpenAI's new "Ultrafast" mode makes GPT-5.6 Sol respond 14 times faster, using a rival's chips.** OpenAI began a limited preview on August 13 of "Ultrafast," a new inference tier that runs GPT-5.6 Sol up to 14 times faster than standard processing, generating as many as 750 output tokens per second. The speed comes from a partnership with Cerebras, whose Wafer-Scale Engine chips keep 44GB of SRAM on-chip specifically to avoid the memory-bandwidth bottlenecks that slow conventional GPU inference. The preview is currently limited to a select group of API customers testing it in production across coding assistants, customer support, financial research, and e-commerce applications where near-real-time responses matter — notable both as a technical feat and as a business signal, since OpenAI is willing to route flagship-model traffic through non-Nvidia silicon from an outside partner to win latency-sensitive enterprise workloads rather than keeping everything on its own infrastructure stack. As agentic and voice applications increasingly depend on low-latency responses, inference speed — not just raw benchmark scores — is emerging as its own competitive battleground among the major labs. Source: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/previewing-ultrafast/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/openai-introduces-ultrafast-a-new-mode-that-makes-gpt-5-6-sol-work-at-14x-the-speed/)

**OpenAI's revenue run-rate blows past $40 billion, doubling in under eight months, as IPO plans firm up.** Bloomberg reported on August 13 that OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate has topped $40 billion, roughly double where it stood at the end of 2025, with President Greg Brockman telling staff internally that the run rate grew more than 20% month-over-month in July alone. The acceleration is being driven by coding-agent products like Codex, workplace tools like ChatGPT Work, subscription growth, and a nascent advertising business layered on top of ChatGPT's consumer base — and the timing isn't incidental, since OpenAI has already filed confidential paperwork for a stock market listing and this kind of revenue trajectory is central to the pitch bankers will make to public investors. It also sharpens the competitive framing against Anthropic, which Bloomberg notes OpenAI is now directly battling for enterprise customers as both companies race toward potential public offerings, and raises the stakes for how the eventual IPO gets priced against a backdrop of roughly $1.5 trillion in AI infrastructure commitments already made across the industry's biggest players. **Databricks rockets to a $190 billion valuation after investors demanded 5x more than it asked for.** Data-and-AI infrastructure giant Databricks closed a $5 billion strategic funding round on August 13 at a $190 billion valuation — its second raise of 2026 — led by Coatue Management, with Blackstone, MGX, T. ## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY: Whitney Wolfe Herd

On February 11, 2021, Whitney Wolfe Herd stood in Bumble's Austin headquarters and rang the Nasdaq opening bell with her one-year-old son propped on her hip. Shares that had priced at $43 closed the day above $70, and by nightfall the 31-year-old founder was worth roughly $1.5 billion — the youngest woman ever to take a company public in the United States, and the youngest self-made female billionaire in history. It was a tidy, cinematic ending to a story that had begun, seven years earlier, with her name being erased from the company she'd helped build.

Wolfe Herd was born July 1, 1989, in Salt Lake City, Utah, to Michael Wolfe, a property developer, and Kelly Wolfe. The family spent a formative stretch living in Paris when she was in sixth grade, and she attended Judge Memorial Catholic High School before enrolling at Southern Methodist University, where she majored in international studies. Even as an undergraduate she was building things: she launched a small business selling bamboo tote bags to raise money for relief efforts after the 2010 Gulf oil spill — an early sign of the marketing instincts that would later prove decisive in Silicon Valley. Those instincts landed her at Hatch Labs, the startup incubator where, in 2012, she became a co-founder and vice president of marketing at a new swiping app called Tinder. It was Wolfe Herd who devised the strategy that turned Tinder from an obscure download into a cultural phenomenon: she toured sorority chapters across the country in person, signing up members and then encouraging fraternities to join to meet them, seeding campus by campus until the app hit critical mass.

Success at Tinder curdled into crisis. Wolfe Herd had been in a relationship with fellow co-founder Justin Mateen, and after it ended, she alleged in a June 2014 lawsuit that he bombarded her with "horrendously sexist, racist, and otherwise inappropriate" texts, called her a "whore" in front of colleagues at a company party, and that CEO Sean Rad dismissed her complaints when she raised them. She further claimed that months earlier, in November 2013, Mateen and Rad had stripped her of her co-founder title altogether, with Mateen reportedly arguing that having a "girl founder" made the company look like "a joke" and would hurt its valuation. The suit named Mateen, Rad, and parent company IAC; it settled for a reported sum just over $1 million in cash and stock, with no admission of wrongdoing. Wolfe Herd left Tinder amid a wave of online harassment so severe she briefly considered abandoning tech altogether and travelled abroad to regroup.

She didn't stay away long. Andrey Andreev, the Russian entrepreneur behind the dating app Badoo, reached out hoping to recruit her as a chief marketing officer. Wolfe Herd countered instead with an idea of her own and the two built it into something pointed: a dating app in which, for heterosexual matches, only women could send the first message, with a 24-hour window before the connection vanished. Launched in December 2014 and backed by Andreev's MagicLab, Bumble inverted the mechanics that had made — and, for her, nearly broken — the category she'd helped invent. The branding leaned into the metaphor: yellow and black, a queen bee at the center, women "in the driver's seat." The company expanded the same first-move logic into Bumble BFF for friendships and Bumble Bizz for professional networking, growing into one of Match Group's few serious rivals. When Andreev sold his stake to Blackstone in 2019 amid unrelated allegations about MagicLab's workplace culture, Wolfe Herd — by then CEO of the combined company — used the moment to negotiate his full exit, taking sole command of the business that bore her original wound and her second act.

The IPO in February 2021 was the vindication that made headlines, but the years after tested her differently. In November 2023, Wolfe Herd announced she would step down as CEO, handing the role to former Slack chief Lidiane Jones effective January 2024 while she moved into the executive chair seat. The transition lasted barely a year: Jones resigned in early 2025 for what the company called personal reasons, and Bumble announced Wolfe Herd would reclaim the CEO title effective mid-March. Months later, in a candid reflection, she admitted she'd had "no intentions of coming back" — until Jones confided that she was overwhelmed, a moment Wolfe Herd described starkly: "I felt like I was looking in a mirror." Both of them, she said, had made the same mistake — "working that extra hour, putting in that extra trip" — the quiet arithmetic of founder burnout that outlasts any IPO bell. She has since thrown the company at a bigger reinvention, unveiling an AI matchmaking assistant called Bee and telling Axios that Bumble will be "saying goodbye to the swipe and hello to something that I believe is revolutionary for the category," aimed at wringing the exhaustion out of an interaction model she herself pioneered a decade earlier. Married since 2017 to Texas energy executive Michael Herd, with two young sons, she remains — twice over now — the person steering the company built out of the moment someone else tried to write her out of the story.
