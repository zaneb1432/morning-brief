## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Soichiro Honda (1906–1991)

Born to a blacksmith in rural Shizuoka, Japan, Honda left school at 15 to apprentice at a Tokyo auto shop, Art Shokai, where he rose to run a branch and began racing. He opened his own shop in 1928 and in the 1930s started making piston rings — and failed his first product: Toyota rejected his rings for poor quality. Rather than quit, he went back to engineering school, was reportedly mocked as a dropout among students, and learned metallurgy and casting until he could make rings to spec. His factory was later damaged by US bombing and a 1945 earthquake; he sold what remained to Toyota.

Postwar, with fuel scarce, he strapped small war-surplus generator engines to bicycles. Demand outran supply, and Honda Motor Co. was founded in 1948. With financial manager **Takeo Fujisawa** — the business half of the partnership — he built the Dream and Super Cub (1958), which became the best-selling motor vehicle in history. In 1959 Honda entered the US with a tiny operation in Los Angeles; its ad line "You meet the nicest people on a Honda" reframed motorcycling for mainstream buyers. He then took on racing, winning the Isle of Man TT, and in 1960s–70s moved into cars; the CVCC engine met US emissions rules without a catalytic converter.

Honda was famous for hands-on shop-floor presence, hatred of hierarchy and family succession (he and Fujisawa retired together in 1973, and no relatives were placed in the company). He called his career "a series of failures" with success being the 1% that rests on the other 99%. **Takeaway for founders:** a rejected product led to deeper craft; an engineer-founder paired with a strong operator built a global company from a bicycle motor.
