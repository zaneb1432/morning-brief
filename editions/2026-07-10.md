## 1. TECH & AI NEWS

**OpenAI Rolls Out GPT-5.6 Family (Sol, Terra, Luna) to All Users**

OpenAI made its GPT-5.6 model family generally available on July 9, 2026, after a limited API/Codex preview since late June. The release introduces a new three-tier naming convention meant to persist across generations: Sol ($5/$30 per million input/output tokens) targets frontier reasoning and long-horizon agentic work in coding, cybersecurity, and science; Terra ($2.50/$15) matches GPT-5.5-class performance at roughly half the cost for everyday use; and Luna ($1/$6) is the fastest, cheapest tier built for high-volume pipelines. The launch also adds more predictable prompt caching with explicit cache breakpoints and a 30-minute minimum cache life, plus a new "ultra mode" for faster complex tasks. This matters because it resets the competitive pricing and capability bar just as xAI, Anthropic, and Google are all shipping or racing to ship rival frontier models in the same week, intensifying a multi-front frontier-model war.
Source: [OpenAI Releases GPT-5.6 (Sol, Terra, Luna): A Three-Tier Model Family With Programmatic Tool Calling](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/09/openai-releases-gpt-5-6-a-three-tier-model-family-with-programmatic-tool-calling/)

**xAI Launches Grok 4.5, Its First Model Since the Cursor Acquisition**

xAI launched Grok 4.5 on July 8, 2026, calling it the company's most intelligent model yet and its first release since SpaceX's roughly $60 billion acquisition of coding startup Cursor in mid-June. Elon Musk described it as "Opus-class" but faster, more token-efficient, and cheaper, pricing it at $2 input / $6 output per million tokens and making it available immediately in Grok Build, Cursor (all plans), and the xAI console — though not yet in the EU. Independent benchmarking from Artificial Analysis ranked it fourth on its Intelligence Index (score of 54), behind Claude Fable 5, GPT-5.5, and Claude Opus 4.8, tempering some of the launch-day hype. The release also sparked a viral debate over political bias in the model's outputs. It matters as a signal that xAI is pivoting hard toward coding/agentic enterprise workloads rather than purely consumer chat, directly challenging Anthropic and OpenAI on developer tooling.
Source: [Introducing Grok 4.5](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-5)

**Apple Loses EU Court Challenge to Digital Markets Act "Gatekeeper" Status**

The EU's Luxembourg-based General Court ruled on July 8, 2026 that it "dismisses all the actions brought by Apple," upholding the European Commission's designation of Apple as a gatekeeper under the Digital Markets Act for iOS and the App Store. The ruling forecloses Apple's attempt to escape DMA obligations such as allowing alternative app stores, third-party payment systems, and sideloading on iOS. Apple can still appeal to the Court of Justice of the EU, the bloc's highest court, but any further appeal would be limited to questions of law rather than a re-examination of facts. This is a major defeat in Apple's multi-year legal fight against European competition regulation and reinforces the EU's position as the most aggressive global regulator of Big Tech platform power, with implications for how Apple must operate its ecosystem across the entire bloc.
Source: [Apple loses major antitrust appeal in Europe, remains a 'gatekeeper'](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/08/apple-loses-major-antitrust-appeal-in-europe-remains-a-gatekeeper/)

The expansion arrived the same week OpenAI merged Codex into a unified "ChatGPT Work" offering, underscoring that the "coding agent wars" between the two labs are now spilling into general knowledge work well beyond programming.

**Reuters: DeepSeek Is Quietly Building Its Own AI Inference Chip**

Reuters reported exclusively on July 8, 2026 that Hangzhou-based DeepSeek — maker of the globally disruptive R1 reasoning model — has spent roughly a year developing its own in-house AI chip, holding discussions with chip-design firms, foundries, and memory suppliers, and quietly hiring semiconductor engineers without public job postings. Nvidia shares fell roughly 1.6-2.2% on the news. ## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY

### The Woman Who Talked Her Way Past a Soldier — and Built America's Biggest Furniture Store

In 1917, a 23-year-old Belarusian woman named Rose Blumkin stood at the Russian-Chinese border with no passport, no visa, and no plan B. She told the guard she was crossing to buy leather for the army and would bring him a bottle of vodka on her way back. He let her through. She never came back with the vodka. Instead, she made her way to Japan, boarded a cargo ship full of peanuts bound for Seattle, and started the second life that would make her one of the most improbable retail legends in American business history.

Rose — universally known later as "Mrs. B" — grew up desperately poor in a village near Babruysk, the daughter of a rabbi and a mother who once boiled water on the stove just so passersby would see steam and think the family had soup to eat. At thirteen she walked eighteen miles barefoot looking for work and was turned away twenty-five times before a dry-goods store finally hired her. She never learned to read. Decades later, running a company with tens of millions in sales, she still couldn't sign her name in cursive — she learned English one word at a time, taught by her own daughter reciting the day's school lessons back to her at the kitchen table.

By the time she reached Omaha in 1919 and reunited with her husband Isadore, Rose had already proven she could out-hustle anyone. In 1937, in her mid-forties, she opened a furniture store in the basement of Isadore's used-clothing shop with $500 in savings — about $12,000 today. Her business philosophy fit on an index card: "Sell cheap and tell the truth." She undercut manufacturers' suggested prices so aggressively that furious competitors sued her for violating fair-trade pricing laws; she beat them in court, and the free publicity turned Nebraska Furniture Mart into a phenomenon. She worked seventy-hour weeks into her nineties and eventually built the largest home-furnishings store in the country under one roof.

Warren Buffett noticed. In 1983 Berkshire Hathaway bought 90% of the business for $60 million — a deal Buffett famously closed with a single question, "Mrs. B, do you owe any money?" and a handshake, no audit, no lawyers in the room. He later called her one of the most impressive businesspeople he'd ever met, on par with any Fortune 500 CEO.

But the story's best twist came after the sale. In 1989, feeling pushed out by family disputes over control of the store, Rose stormed out at age 95 — and three months later opened a rival store, Mrs. B's Clearance and Factory Outlet, directly across the street from the company she'd just sold. She hadn't signed a non-compete. By 1991 her scrappy new outlet was Omaha's third-largest carpet retailer, and an exasperated but admiring Buffett bought her out a second time in 1992, later joking it was a mistake he'd never repeat. She kept working at Nebraska Furniture Mart until 103 and died in 1998 at 104, having spent nearly nine decades proving that neither poverty, illiteracy, nor retirement itself could slow her down.
