## TECH & AI NEWS

**Nvidia agrees to buy Hugging Face for $12.93 billion.** Nvidia confirmed it will acquire Hugging Face, the platform that hosts roughly three million models, half a million datasets, and a million applications used by more than 18 million developers across 200,000+ companies. The deal — about $11.9 billion to investors plus up to $1 billion in employee retention — is Nvidia's second-largest acquisition on record. It matters because it hands the company that already dominates AI compute a commanding position over AI software distribution too: Jensen Huang has pledged Hugging Face will "remain an open platform" and keep supporting rival hardware from AMD and Intel, but regulators and competitors will be watching closely whether that promise holds once the world's default model hub answers to the world's dominant chipmaker. Notably, Hugging Face reportedly approached Huang about the deal, not the other way around. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/nvidia-confirms-it-will-buy-hugging-face-for-12-9-billion/)

**OpenAI launches GPT-6 Astra, its most capable model yet.** Astra began rolling out September 3 to enterprise customers in OpenAI's Daybreak cybersecurity program, with API, AWS, and ChatGPT Plus/Pro/Business/Enterprise access following in the days after. The headline technical change is "recurrent depth," a reasoning technique that lets the model loop over a problem internally rather than writing out every step of its chain of thought — VP of research Aidan Clark called the training run "by far" OpenAI's largest, the first pretrained on more than 100,000 GPUs at its Stargate site in Texas. The catch: because recurrent depth obscures some or all of the model's visible reasoning, safety researchers at Redwood Research and elsewhere have flagged it as a real complication for AI oversight, even as OpenAI points to added misalignment monitoring and a staged rollout as mitigations. It's a preview of a coming industry tension — more capable reasoning bought at the cost of less legible reasoning. Source: [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3226859/openais-astra-model-has-ai-researchers-spooked-heres-why.html)

**Moonshot AI files confidentially for a Hong Kong IPO targeting a $50 billion valuation.** The Chinese AI lab is seeking roughly $3 billion in the offering, with underwriters Goldman Sachs, CICC, and Deutsche Bank. The valuation jump — from about $30 billion just a month ago — follows the July release of its flagship Kimi K3 model, and the company has already restructured from an offshore holding company into an onshore China domicile to clear the way for the listing, which could come as early as year-end. It matters as a bellwether for how public markets will price frontier AI labs outside the US, and as a sign that Chinese AI companies are moving to lock in IPO windows while investor appetite for the sector remains strong. Source: [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3363026/chinas-moonshot-ai-aims-us50b-round-year-end-hong-kong-ipo-targeted-sources)

**Broadcom's AI chip revenue triples as it guides toward $115 billion in fiscal 2027.** Broadcom's fiscal Q3 revenue jumped 86% to $29.6 billion, with AI-chip sales up 221% year-over-year to $16.7 billion. Management guided to $21.7 billion in AI revenue for Q4, and projected roughly $115 billion for fiscal 2027 and $230 billion for fiscal 2028 — yet the stock fell anyway because the overall Q4 revenue guide of $34.8 billion came in slightly under the $35.03 billion Wall Street wanted. The reaction is the story here: even blowout AI-chip growth is no longer enough on its own, a sign investor expectations for AI infrastructure spending have become extremely demanding. Source: [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/02/broadcom-earnings-ai-chip-sales-tripled-here-s-the-usd34-8-billion-number-investors-need-to-watch/)

**Equinix, Nvidia, and Together AI team up to launch an "Inference Exchange."** Unveiled September 2 and targeting a Q1 2027 launch, the service layers Equinix's data-center and interconnect infrastructure, Nvidia's enterprise reference architecture for high-throughput AI compute, and Together AI's inference-serving software (which supports 200+ open models) into a single distributed offering. The pitch is to let enterprises run inference physically closer to their own data and users rather than routing everything through hyperscaler clouds — a direct bet that "inference at the edge" becomes as important a battleground as training compute has been, and a sign that colocation providers see an opening to become AI infrastructure players in their own right. Source: [Equinix Newsroom](https://newsroom.equinix.com/2026-09-02-Equinix-Accelerates-AI-Inference-for-Enterprises-with-NVIDIA-and-Together-AI)

## STARTUP WORLD

**Félix Pago raises $200 million to expand beyond remittances.** The Miami-based, WhatsApp-native remittance platform for Latino immigrants closed a Series C combining $87 million in equity — led by Andreessen Horowitz with QED Investors, Castle Island Ventures, and others — and a $113 million credit facility from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund, at a $1.4 billion valuation. Founded in 2020 by Wharton alumni Manuel Godoy and Bernardo García, Félix says it has processed more than $8 billion in transfers for six million-plus users and will use the new capital to push into lending and savings products. It's a strong signal that investors still see room to build full financial-services stacks on top of chat-app distribution in underbanked, immigrant-heavy markets. Source: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/felix-pago-raises-200-million-to-expand-into-financial-services)

**HyImpulse secures over €50 million to scale European rocket launches.** The German spacetech company closed a Series A extension co-led by JOIN Capital and Ace Capital Partners, with new backing from North Ventures, Bayern Kapital, and Germany's national aerospace center (DLR), bringing total funding past €125 million. HyImpulse says it already has a €350 million-plus order book for its suborbital and orbital launch programs and will use the money toward a second SR75 suborbital launch from Scotland's SaxaVord Spaceport and the maiden flight of its orbital vehicle, SL1. The round underlines a broader European push — with direct government backing — to build sovereign launch capability rather than depend on SpaceX or other non-European providers. Source: [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/09/german-spacetech-hyimpulse-secures-over-e50-million-to-accelerate-europes-sovereign-launch-capabilities)

**Gridsight raises $26 million to help utilities find hidden grid capacity.** The Series B was led by Insight Partners, with Galvanize joining existing investors Airtree, Energy Transition Ventures, and Aera VC, bringing the AI grid-capacity startup's total funding to about $33.5 million. Gridsight's software helps utilities — including Xcel Energy, United Illuminating in the US and Endeavour Energy in Australia — figure out how much spare capacity already exists on their distribution networks rather than defaulting to expensive new build-out. The round is a good proxy for a fast-growing category: AI-driven data centers and EV charging are straining grids everywhere, and "software before steel" is becoming its own investable thesis in energy infrastructure. Source: [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/gridsight-raises-26-million-to-help-utilities-unlock-grid-capacity-with-ai/)

**Ex-Palantir founders raise €5 million for Zeit AI, an "autonomous data engineer" for Europe's mid-market.** The Munich-based, Y Combinator-backed startup — founded by Leopold von Waldthausen and Marvin Bornstein — connects ERP, CRM, and 600-plus other source systems and uses an AI agent to build analytics-ready applications from them, with data kept in German data centers. The round drew an unusually eclectic cap table, including Sequoia's Scout Fund, Hasso Plattner (SAP's founder), Meta board member Charlie Songhurst, and even footballer Mario Götze and former German finance minister Christian Lindner as angels. It's a small deal, but it captures a real trend: European "sovereign AI" pitches — AI infrastructure that keeps data and compute inside the EU — are pulling in both institutional and celebrity capital as data-residency politics sharpen. Source: [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/09/yc-backed-zeit-ai-raises-e5-million-to-build-europes-autonomous-data-engineer)

## VENTURE CAPITAL

**a16z launches a $1.1 billion "Machine Age Fund" dedicated to AI's physical infrastructure.** Announced August 28 by general partners Ben Horowitz, Martin Casado, Raghu Raghuram, David Ulevitch, and David George, the fund targets chips, memory, networking, systems software, power, and robotics — the physical layer AI now runs on — rather than model or application-layer startups. The firm's stated thesis: the binding constraint on AI progress has shifted from model quality to the physical capacity to build and power the machines that run it. Days later, on August 31, a16z separately expanded its growth fund to $8.5 billion, underscoring just how much capital the firm is stacking up specifically for the compute/hardware buildout phase of the AI cycle rather than early-stage software bets. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/28/a16z-creates-a-1-1b-machine-age-fund-to-accelerate-the-physical-buildout-of-ai/)

**General Catalyst extends venture debt, not just equity, into fintech.** In the Félix Pago round above, General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund put in $113 million as a credit facility rather than an equity check — a reminder that some of the largest multi-stage firms are increasingly using debt vehicles to get bigger, less-dilutive exposure to fast-growing but capital-intensive startups (in this case, a remittance company that has to hold and move real money). It's a small but telling structural shift in how "venture capital" actually deploys capital at scale in 2026. Source: [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/fintech-whatsapp-remittance-startup-felix-raises-200m-a16z-general-catalyst/)

**Base10 Partners leads a repeat bet on insurtech AI startup Axle.** Base10, joined by Gradient Ventures (Google's AI fund), Y Combinator, Stage 2 Capital, and Plaid's founders as angels, led a $17.5 million Series A for Axle AI, which automates insurance-policy workflows. Gradient Ventures and Y Combinator had also backed Axle's $4 million seed, making this one of several recent rounds where the same investor syndicate doubles down quickly as a vertical-AI startup shows traction — a pattern increasingly common in narrow, workflow-specific AI applications rather than horizontal model plays. Source: [Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/ai-insurance-startup-axle-raises-17-5m-from-base10-partners-to-automate-policy-workflows/)

## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY: An Wang

Long before Silicon Valley immigrant-founder stories became a familiar genre, there was An Wang. Born in Shanghai in 1920, Wang was a math prodigy who entered the elite Shanghai Provincial High School at 13 and graduated from Chiao Tung University — then known as "the MIT of China" — in 1940. When Japan occupied Shanghai during World War II, Wang fled to Guilin, where he led a small team that scavenged spare parts and improvised radio designs for the Chinese military. In 1945 he immigrated to the United States, earning a PhD in applied physics from Harvard, where he made a foundational contribution to computing: he invented a method for magnetic core memory, a technology that became the standard for storing data in computers for nearly two decades.

When Harvard shut down its computer research program in 1951, Wang struck out on his own, founding Wang Laboratories as a one-person operation making and selling magnetic cores. In 1955 he sold his core-memory patent to IBM and poured the proceeds into building a company that would eventually compete directly with the giant that had just paid him. Wang Labs found its footing first in calculators, then made a decisive pivot into word processors — a bet that turned the company into one of the dominant office-technology firms of the 1970s and '80s. At its peak, Wang Laboratories generated more than $2.5 billion in annual revenue and employed tens of thousands of people, a remarkable arc for an immigrant who had arrived in America a decade earlier with little more than a physics education and wartime engineering experience.

Wang's downfall is as instructive as his rise. When he stepped back from day-to-day leadership in 1986, he insisted — over the objections of his board — on handing the company to his son, Fred Wang, rather than choosing the most qualified outside executive. The succession faltered as the word-processor market gave way to the general-purpose PC, a shift Wang Labs failed to navigate, and An Wang was forced to remove his own son from the CEO role in 1989. He died of cancer in March 1990; the company he built filed for bankruptcy just two years later, in 1992. An Wang's story endures as one of the great cautionary tales in tech: a brilliant inventor and builder whose reluctance to separate family loyalty from succession planning erased, within a few years, an empire that had taken four decades to build. Sources: [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/An-Wang), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Wang), [Computer History Museum](https://history.computer.org/pioneers/wang.html)
