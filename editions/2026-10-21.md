## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

Sarah Breedlove was born in Louisiana to formerly enslaved parents, orphaned at seven, married at 14, and widowed by 20 with a young daughter, A'Lelia. She worked as a washerwoman in St. Louis for about $1.50 a day. In the early 1900s, suffering hair loss from scalp disease, she began experimenting with remedies, partly after working as a sales agent for Annie Turnbo Malone's hair-care company. Moving to Denver in 1905, she married newspaperman Charles Joseph Walker and rebranded as "Madam C.J. Walker," launching her own line of scalp treatments and haircare for Black women.

Her real innovation was distribution. She sold door to door, trained a network of "beauty culturists" in her own system, and built Lelia College and a manufacturing base in Indianapolis from 1910. Commissioned agents, local clubs and mail order scaled the business to thousands of women, giving many an income independent of domestic labor. She reinvested in advertising centered on dignity and self-reliance, and became a major philanthropist: funding the NAACP's anti-lynching efforts, the YMCA and Tuskegee, and publicly protesting after the 1917 East St. Louis riot. She is often described as America's first self-made woman millionaire, a claim that is debated but widely repeated; she certainly built one of the largest Black-owned businesses of her era. She died in 1919 at 51. Lesson for founders: build the distribution system and train the people, not just the product.
