## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

Sarah Breedlove was born on a Louisiana cotton plantation in December 1867, the first child in her family born free after Emancipation. Orphaned at seven, married at 14, and widowed by 20 with a young daughter, A'Lelia, she worked as a washerwoman in St. Louis for roughly $1.50 a day, saving to send A'Lelia to school.

In the 1890s she suffered severe scalp ailments and hair loss, common among women then because of poor sanitation and harsh products. She experimented with remedies and worked as a sales agent for Annie Malone's Poro Company. In 1905 she moved to Denver, married newspaper salesman Charles J. Walker, and began selling her own hair-care system as "Madam C.J. Walker", a name that doubled as a brand. She credited the formula to a dream; whatever its origin, she was a tireless demonstrator, going door to door, then town to town across the South and East.

Her real innovation was distribution. Rather than sell through stores, she trained a sales force of "Walker Agents", tens of thousands of Black women, in her system of products, grooming standards and bookkeeping, at a time when few paying careers were open to them. She founded Lelia College to train "hair culturists," opened a factory in Indianapolis in 1910, and held conventions that recognized top agents with prizes, including one of the first national gatherings of Black women entrepreneurs. Her company also pioneered mail-order sales and aggressive advertising in the Black press.

She reinvested in community and politics: major gifts to the NAACP's anti-lynching campaign, the YMCA, Tuskegee Institute, and scholarships; she also helped fund the Mary McLeod Bethune-aligned causes and joined a delegation to the White House to protest lynching. In 1917 she built a Hudson Valley estate, Villa Lewaro, designed by Black architect Vertner Tandy. She died in May 1919 at about 51, widely reported as one of America's first self-made female millionaires (the exact net worth is debated, with estimates typically around $600,000+ in 1919 dollars).

**Lesson for founders:** build the channel, not just the product, and let the channel's participants win with you.
