## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

Born Sarah Breedlove in 1867 near Delta, Louisiana, to parents who had been enslaved, she was orphaned at seven and married at 14. Widowed young, she moved to St. Louis, where her brothers were barbers, and worked for over a decade as a washerwoman for very little pay. Hair loss pushed her to develop a scalp and hair-growth treatment. Around 1905 (sources vary between 1904 and 1905) she began selling it in Denver, taking the name Madam C.J. Walker.

Her edge was distribution. She sold door to door, advertised in Black newspapers, and built a network of trained sales agents, "Walker hair culturists." In 1908 she opened Lelia College in Pittsburgh to train them, and in 1910 she moved to Indianapolis and built a factory. By her death on May 25, 1919, her schools had trained more than 40,000 women as sales agents, giving many a path to financial independence. In New York she became active in politics and philanthropy.

She is often called America's first self-made female millionaire, but some sources question that: her estate at death was reportedly about $600,000 (roughly $8M today). Either way, the lessons hold for founders today: she solved her own problem, built a trained sales network before "network marketing" was a term, and put her profits into her community.
