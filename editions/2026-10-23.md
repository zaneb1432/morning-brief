## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

*(No prior editions existed in the repo, so no repeats to avoid. Written from general historical knowledge, not live research.)*

Sarah Breedlove was born in Louisiana on a former cotton plantation, the first child in her family born free after Emancipation. She was orphaned at seven, married at 14 to escape an abusive situation, and widowed at 20 with a young daughter, A'Lelia. She worked as a laundress in St. Louis for about $1.50 a day, and in the 1890s she began losing her hair to scalp disease, common at the time because of poor sanitation, harsh products and infrequent washing.

She worked for a time as a sales agent for Annie Malone's Poro hair-care company. In 1905 she moved to Denver, and in 1906 she married newspaper advertising salesman Charles J. Walker. She took the name "Madam C.J. Walker" and launched her own line, including a scalp treatment she said came to her in a dream. Her real edge was distribution and brand-building. She sold door to door, trained women as "beauty culturists," and in 1908 opened a Pittsburgh college and factory. In 1910 she moved to Indianapolis, building the Walker Manufacturing Company. Her sales force eventually numbered in the thousands, giving Black women a path to economic independence at a time when few options existed. She emphasized scalp health and grooming and used before-and-after photos and her own image heavily in marketing.

She was also a philanthropist and activist. She gave to the NAACP's anti-lynching efforts, the YMCA and Black education, and she helped fund Mary McLeod Bethune's school. She spoke out after the 1917 East St. Louis riot. She died in 1919 at 51. She is often described as the first self-made female millionaire in America, though that claim is debated. Her fortune is commonly estimated at over $600,000 at her death (millions in today's dollars).

**Lessons:** Build the distribution engine, not just the product. Turn customers into a sales force. Use the wealth you build to widen the market for others.
