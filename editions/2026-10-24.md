## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Margaret Rudkin (Pepperidge Farm)

*(Written from background knowledge, not from today's searches; this is the first edition, so there are no repeats.)*

Margaret Rudkin (1897–1967) was a Manhattan-born, Wall Street-married housewife who built one of America's best-known bread and baked-goods brands out of her Connecticut kitchen. In the mid-1930s she and her husband Henry, a stockbroker, lived on a Fairfield, Connecticut estate they named Pepperidge Farm after the pepperidge (black gum) trees on the property. Her youngest son suffered from severe allergies and asthma, and a doctor suggested he avoid commercial bread's additives. Rudkin began baking stone-ground whole-wheat bread from natural ingredients for him.

The loaves impressed the doctor and neighbors, and she began selling to a local grocer, then expanded as demand outran her home oven. Her pitch was a premium product at a premium price, at a time when cheap, uniform white bread dominated. Wealthy customers and specialty grocers took to it, and she scaled from a home operation to a dedicated bakery and then a factory. She was known for strict ingredient standards and personally overseeing quality, and she later wrote a cookbook that kept the brand in the public mind.

In 1961 she sold Pepperidge Farm to Campbell Soup Company in a stock deal, and stayed on as head of the business. She is often cited as an early example of a woman building a national consumer brand in an era when few women led large companies. Founder lesson: she started from a specific, personal problem, refused to compete on price, and let product quality build the brand before scaling it.
