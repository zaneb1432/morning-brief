## 1. TECH & AI NEWS

**OpenAI ships GPT-6 and "Intelligent UI" in ChatGPT.** On October 7, OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 in ChatGPT's Chat tab. ## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Masaru Ibuka (1908–1997), co-founder of Sony

*From memory, not from this run's searches. No prior editions exist in this repo, so no repeat.*

Masaru Ibuka was an engineer first. Born in Nikko, Japan, he studied engineering at Waseda University and built a reputation as a gifted inventor in the 1930s. He then worked in wartime research, where he met Akio Morita, a young naval engineer.

In September 1945, weeks after Japan's surrender, Ibuka opened a small radio repair shop in a bombed-out Tokyo department store building. In 1946 he and Morita formally founded Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo ("Tokyo Telecommunications Engineering") with about 20 employees and roughly ¥190,000 in capital. Ibuka's founding prospectus said he wanted to build a "place of work where engineers can feel the joy of technology" and put their skills to work for society.

Early products failed. An electric rice cooker did not sell. The first real hit was Japan's first magnetic tape recorder, in 1950. Ibuka and Morita then licensed transistor technology from Western Electric in 1953. Ibuka pushed to use it for a small radio rather than for hearing aids, and in 1955 the company shipped Japan's first transistor radio. The company took the name Sony in 1958, a name easy to say in any language.

Ibuka was the technical visionary, and Morita was the salesman and global face. Their later products included the Trinitron television (1968). In 1979 Ibuka asked engineers for a small stereo cassette player he could use on flights. That became the Walkman, which Morita championed against internal skepticism.

Ibuka was also an educator at heart. In 1971 he wrote *Kindergarten Is Too Late*, arguing for early childhood development, and he later funded work on it. He died in December 1997.

**Lessons:** Pair a technical founder with a commercial partner. Treat early failures as tuition. Chase a product you personally want.
