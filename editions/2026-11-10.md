## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

(Written from background knowledge; no prior editions exist in this repo, so no repeats.)

Sarah Breedlove was born on a Louisiana cotton plantation to formerly enslaved parents, orphaned at seven, married at 14, and widowed by 20 with a young daughter, A'Lelia. She worked as a laundress for about $1.50 a day. In the early 1900s, suffering scalp disease and hair loss, she worked for Annie Malone's Poro Company as a sales agent, then developed her own scalp-treatment formula. Reinventing herself as "Madam C.J." after her third husband, Charles Joseph Walker, she moved to Denver in 1905 and started selling door to door, building a business model unusually modern for its time: a manufacturing core plus a commissioned army of "beauty culturists" trained in the "Walker System." By 1910 she had relocated to Indianapolis, built a factory, and founded a training college. Her agents, mostly Black women, gained a path to independent income, and she funded the NAACP anti-lynching campaign, the YMCA, and scholarships. At her death in 1919 she was widely reported to be the wealthiest self-made Black woman in America. Lesson for founders: she built distribution and training, not just product, and treated her sales force as a movement.
