## TECH & AI NEWS

**OpenAI's Enterprise Business Overtakes Consumer Revenue, Two Quarters Ahead of Schedule**
On August 14, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar told investors that enterprise revenue has now surpassed the company's consumer ChatGPT business for the first time — "the majority of our revenue is now enterprise," she said, noting the company entered the year at a 60/40 consumer-enterprise split but "those lines have now crossed." OpenAI's annualized recurring revenue hit $40 billion, up 20% month-over-month in July, with business-customer count growing 32% in the same period. This moves up a milestone OpenAI had projected for "parity by end of 2026" by roughly two quarters when the company closed its $122 billion funding round in March. The shift signals that enterprise adoption — not consumer chat usage — is now the primary growth engine for the industry's biggest AI lab, a data point that will matter heavily as OpenAI prepares for its widely anticipated IPO.
Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/openai-cfo-friar-tells-investors-that-enterprise-bigger-than-consumer.html)

**Anthropic's Quarterly Revenue Surges Past $11.5 Billion, Posts First Operating Profit**
Anthropic's preliminary Q2 2026 revenue exceeded $11.5 billion, according to documents reviewed by Bloomberg and reported August 14–15 — a more than 14-fold increase from the $787 million it made in the same quarter a year earlier, and up sharply from the $4.73 billion it reported in Q1 2026. Crucially, the company posted positive adjusted operating income for the first time, a notable milestone for a lab that has burned enormous amounts of capital on model training and compute. The numbers arrive as Anthropic prepares for a potential IPO and continues to compete head-to-head with OpenAI for enterprise customers, particularly in coding and agentic-tool markets. The scale and speed of this growth — alongside OpenAI's similar enterprise surge — underscore how quickly frontier AI labs are converting from cash-burning research operations into major revenue-generating businesses.
Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/15/anthropic-revenue-jumps-to-over-11point5-billion-in-q2-report.html)

**Nvidia Discloses $21 Billion SpaceX Stake and $30 Billion Intel Position**
In a Friday, August 14 regulatory filing, Nvidia revealed it held roughly 122.8 million shares of SpaceX worth about $21 billion as of June 30, and 214.8 million Intel shares worth around $30 billion — the first time Nvidia has disclosed the value of its SpaceX position since investing in Elon Musk's xAI (later folded into SpaceX ahead of its public debut earlier this year). The Intel stake represents a massive return on Nvidia's roughly $5 billion investment made less than a year ago. Together, the SpaceX and Intel positions now account for nearly 80% of Nvidia's publicly disclosed equity portfolio, making Nvidia not just the dominant AI chip supplier but increasingly a major strategic investor across the compute and space industries it depends on as customers.
Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/nvidia-discloses-21-billion-stake-in-spacex-at-end-of-second-quarter.html)

**Alibaba's Qwen Models Cross 3 Billion Downloads, Overtaking Google and Meta in Open-Source AI**
Bloomberg reported August 15 that Alibaba's Qwen family of open-weight AI models has surpassed 3 billion global downloads in roughly six months, outpacing every other major open-source AI ecosystem, including Google (about 418 million downloads in 2026) and Meta's Llama (227 million), according to Hugging Face data. The Qwen ecosystem now spans more than 460 open-source models and 300,000 derivative fine-tunes, capturing over half of all open-source model downloads worldwide; a new flagship, Qwen3.8-Max (a 2.4-trillion-parameter model), is slated for open-weight release later this month. This is a significant signal that Chinese AI labs — not just Meta — have become the dominant force in open-weight model distribution, reshaping assumptions about where the open-source AI center of gravity sits.
Source: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/alibaba-ai-models-hit-3-billion-downloads-passing-meta-google)

**China's Z.ai Releases GLM-5.3, Withholds Weights Over Emergent Cybersecurity Capabilities**
Chinese AI lab Z.ai launched GLM-5.3 on August 14, an open-weights update that keeps the same base architecture as GLM-5.2 but scales up post-training to deliver its strongest-ever coding performance, alongside cybersecurity capabilities that reportedly grew faster than the company anticipated during training. The model scored 84.5% on CyberGym (a benchmark for identifying and validating vulnerabilities from source code), up from 77.2% for its predecessor and ahead of every model in Z.ai's comparison set; it has reportedly already found a "serious vulnerability" in the coding tool Cursor. Because of the risk, Z.ai is withholding the model weights for two weeks pending additional safety hardening and restricting the most sensitive cyber functions to a "trusted access" verification program. It's one of the clearer recent examples of a lab explicitly slow-walking its own release over dual-use security risk, putting pressure on the broader open-weights ecosystem to treat offensive-cyber capability as a first-class safety concern.
Source: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/14/china-open-source-ai-glm-53)

## STARTUP WORLD

**SpaceX Officially Closes Its $60 Billion Cursor Acquisition**
SpaceX completed its all-stock acquisition of AI coding startup Cursor on August 15, closing a deal first structured as an option in April and converted into a firm commitment in June, just after SpaceX's blockbuster Nasdaq IPO. Cursor, founded by Michael Truell, is folding its engineering team into SpaceX's internal software division, and in its own announcement emphasized it now gets "access to the largest fleet of GPUs in the world," including SpaceX's Colossus supercomputer. This is one of the largest acquisitions of an AI startup to date and marks Elon Musk's second major AI-coding/compute consolidation move after xAI, signaling that rocket and satellite infrastructure players now see proprietary compute as a competitive weapon in the AI coding-tools race against Anthropic and OpenAI.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/15/spacex-officially-closes-its-cursor-acquisition/)

**Marco Arment Launches Unforgetful, a Paid Alternative to Apple Reminders**
On August 14, Marco Arment — the indie developer behind Overcast and Instapaper, and Tumblr's former CTO — launched Unforgetful, a new iOS/Mac app built to fix what he sees as core flaws in Apple's Reminders app: alerts that silently vanish, dismissed notifications that never return, and inflexible snoozing. He describes it as "Reminders for ADHD"; it works against your existing Reminders database and adds persistent repeating notifications, scaling snooze intervals, widgets, and voice input, priced at $19.99/year with a one-month free trial. It's a small but notable example of a solo/indie founder shipping a polished, monetizable niche productivity tool in a market otherwise dominated by AI-agent hype.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/unforgetful-is-a-new-reminders-app-for-people-who-cant-stop-hitting-snooze/)

**Paris Startup Kog Claims 30x Faster LLM Inference on Existing GPUs**
TechCrunch profiled Paris-based Kog on August 14, a small AI-infrastructure startup (11 people, founded 2023 by Gaël Delalleau, previously raised $5M from Varsity VC and BPI France) that says pure software optimization — not new chips — can unlock up to 30x faster large-language-model inference on standard datacenter GPUs. On a single node of eight AMD MI300X GPUs, Kog claims it can generate more than 3,000 output tokens per second for a single user request, well beyond what most inference stacks achieve on the same hardware. The story matters because it challenges the industry's default assumption that faster AI requires ever-more-expensive next-gen chips, offering a cheaper path to inference speed and a European deep-tech challenger in a field dominated by well-funded US inference startups.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/kog-is-going-deeper-to-squeeze-more-inference-out-of-gpus/)

**Thrive Capital's Joshua Kushner Warns Silicon Valley VCs Against AI "Euphoria"**
In Thrive Capital's first-ever formal investor letter, published/reported August 14, founder Joshua Kushner — whose firm now manages roughly $60 billion in assets — took a rare public swipe at West Coast venture peers, warning that "in moments of euphoria, investors tend to convince themselves that second- and third-tier assets are actually first-tier" and that the industry risks becoming "fixated on hyperincremental technological turns rather than where the technology ultimately leads." Kushner disclosed a 41% gross IRR and 33% net IRR across Thrive's funds, plus over $1 billion in liquidity returned to investors in the past year, even as he doubled down on AI as a generational opportunity. It's a rare instance of a top AI-bullish investor publicly cautioning about froth in startup valuations — the same week Databricks priced a round investors bid up 3x beyond what the company asked for (see Venture Capital, below).
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/thrives-joshua-kushner-chides-silicon-valley-vcs-over-ai-euphoria/)

## VENTURE CAPITAL

**Databricks Closes a Record $5 Billion Round at a $190 Billion Valuation**
Data and AI infrastructure company Databricks closed a $5 billion strategic funding round on August 13, valuing the company at $190 billion — up sharply from its prior mark and making it one of the most valuable private companies in the world. The round was led by Coatue, with Blackstone, MGX, T. Rowe Price and new investor Sixth Street Growth also participating, alongside existing backers Andreessen Horowitz and Thrive Capital. Databricks originally sought to raise just $1 billion, but investor demand pushed the deal toward $15 billion before the company settled on $5 billion — CEO Ali Ghodsi told TechCrunch "my phone blew up" once word got out. The company justified the valuation with a $7 billion-plus annualized revenue run rate (up more than 80% year-over-year) and positive adjusted cash flow for twelve straight months. The deal signals that mega-round enterprise AI infrastructure bets remain the dominant use of VC dollars in 2026, with top-tier crossover and growth investors competing hard for allocation in proven, revenue-generating AI platforms.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/databricks-wanted-to-raise-1b-investors-wanted-15b-it-settled-on-5b-at-a-190b-valuation/)

## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY

### Chuck Feeney

On a warm night in the French Riviera town of Villefranche-sur-Mer, sometime in the mid-1950s, a 26-year-old Cornell graduate named Chuck Feeney struck up a bar conversation that would eventually reshape American philanthropy. Feeney — the son of an insurance man and a nurse from Elizabeth, New Jersey, and the first in his Irish Catholic family to attend college, courtesy of the GI Bill after four years in the Air Force during Korea — was scraping by selling encyclopedias and teaching English. The town happened to host the U.S. Sixth Fleet, and Feeney noticed something: sailors could buy up to five bottles of duty-free liquor and ship them home as "unaccompanied baggage," turning a $10 case into a $30 windfall. An Englishman named Bob Edmonds had spotted the same loophole. Feeney jumped in, then recruited a Cornell classmate a year ahead of him, Robert Miller, as a partner. By 1960 the two had formalized the hustle into Duty Free Shoppers Group, hawking not just liquor but cigarettes, perfume, and eventually cars to American servicemen and, later, Japanese tourists pouring into Hawaii and Hong Kong.

DFS became one of the great hidden fortunes of the twentieth century — a private company that eventually sold nearly $3 billion a year in goods to travelers, minting Feeney a fortune estimated at $1.3 billion or more by the 1980s. And that's where his story turns from a good business tale into something almost nobody saw coming: in 1982, at the height of his wealth, Feeney secretly transferred nearly his entire stake in DFS into a foundation called Atlantic Philanthropies — then told no one. Not his employees, not most of his family, not even the charities receiving his checks, which arrived through intermediaries and demanded anonymity as a condition of every gift. He kept flying economy, wearing a $15 watch, carrying his papers in a plastic bag, and living in rented apartments, all while quietly funding causes from Cornell's campus to the Northern Ireland peace process to South Africa after apartheid — over $8 billion in total.

The secret held for fifteen years, until a business dispute forced his hand: when Feeney sold his DFS interests to LVMH in 1996, his falling-out with Robert Miller over the deal was headed for court, threatening to expose the arrangement through litigation. Rather than let it leak out sideways, Feeney called The New York Times himself. The resulting 1997 story, "He Gave Away $600 Million and No One Knew," blew his cover for good and turned him into what Forbes later dubbed "the James Bond of philanthropy."

What makes Feeney's story worth telling isn't just the scale of the giving — it's the philosophy behind it, which he called "Giving While Living." He believed dying rich was a failure of imagination, that money does more good funding a cause today than sitting in an endowment for decades. By 2020 he'd achieved his explicit goal: giving away essentially everything, right down to closing Atlantic Philanthropies' doors on purpose. When he died in 2023 at 92, his net worth was under $2 million — a man who built one empire in duty-free shops and then spent forty years quietly dismantling the other empire, his own fortune, on purpose.
