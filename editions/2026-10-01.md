## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

Sarah Breedlove was born on a Louisiana cotton plantation in 1867, the first child in her family born free after emancipation. Orphaned at seven, married at 14, and widowed at 20 with a young daughter, A'Lelia, she worked as a washerwoman in St. Louis for about $1.50 a day, determined to educate her child.

In the 1890s she began losing her hair to scalp disease, a common affliction caused by poor sanitation, harsh products and infrequent washing. She worked for a time as a sales agent for Annie Malone's Poro Company, then developed her own scalp-treatment formula. In 1906 she moved to Denver, married newspaper sales agent Charles J. Walker, and took the name Madam C.J. Walker, building a brand around her "Wonderful Hair Grower."

Her genius was distribution. She sold door to door, then recruited and trained thousands of Black women as "beauty culturists," giving them a path to independence at a time when domestic service was the default. She founded Lelia College to train them, ran national conventions, and built a manufacturing base in Indianapolis from 1910. Her company was among the era's most prominent Black-owned businesses, and she is often cited as one of the first self-made female millionaires in America, a claim historians note is debated by definition, but her wealth was real.

She used it for philanthropy and activism: major gifts to the NAACP's anti-lynching campaign, support for the YMCA and Tuskegee, and public criticism of racial violence, including a delegation to the White House after the 1917 East St. Louis riot. She died in 1919 at 51, leaving two-thirds of future company profits to charity.

**Lesson for founders:** she paired a product with a distribution and training system that created her own channel and community, a model later echoed in direct-selling and creator platforms.
