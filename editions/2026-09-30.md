## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

*First edition, so no prior founders to avoid repeating.*

Sarah Breedlove was born on December 23, 1867, on a Louisiana cotton plantation, the first in her family born free after emancipation. Orphaned at seven, married at fourteen and widowed by twenty, she worked as a laundress for about $1.50 a day while raising her daughter, A'Lelia.

In the 1890s a scalp ailment caused her to lose much of her hair. She experimented with remedies, and after working as a sales agent for another Black hair-care entrepreneur, Annie Malone, she moved to Denver, married Charles Joseph Walker, and launched "Madam Walker's Wonderful Hair Grower." In 1908 she formally established the Madam C.J. Walker Manufacturing Company.

Her real innovation was distribution. Rather than rely on shops, she trained a national force of "Walker Agents," Black women who sold the products door to door and learned hygiene, grooming and bookkeeping. It gave thousands of women a path to independent income at a time when almost none existed. She moved operations to Indianapolis in 1910, built a factory and training school, and invested in real estate. She is widely described as the first female self-made millionaire in the US.

She spent heavily on causes she cared about: the NAACP's anti-lynching campaign, Black colleges and community institutions. She died on May 25, 1919, at 51. The takeaway for founders: she built a brand, a manufacturing base and a distribution network in one company, and she treated her sales channel as a mission.
