## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Margaret Rudkin (Pepperidge Farm)

*No prior editions exist in this repo, so there is nothing to repeat. Written from general knowledge, not web research; confirm specifics before quoting.*

Margaret Rudkin (1897–1967) is a lesser-known founder of a very well-known brand. Raised in New York, she worked on Wall Street and married stockbroker Henry Rudkin. In the 1920s and '30s the couple moved to a farm in Fairfield, Connecticut, which they named Pepperidge Farm after the pepperidge (black gupta) trees on the property.

The business began in 1937 as a family problem. Her youngest son had severe allergies and asthma, and his doctor suggested he avoid commercial bread's additives. Rudkin began baking stone-ground whole-wheat bread from natural ingredients at home. When the family doctor and neighbors liked it, she began selling loaves to local grocers, famously fighting to convince them that a premium bread priced well above the standard loaf could sell.

She scaled from the farm kitchen to a bakery, kept an uncompromising quality line (natural ingredients, no preservatives), and added products such as cookies and frozen goods. Pepperidge Farm became a national brand, and in 1961 she sold it to Campbell Soup Company in a stock deal valued at roughly $28M, staying on as an executive. She also wrote *The Margaret Rudkin Pepperidge Farm Cookbook* (1963).

The lesson: a founder-market fit born from a personal problem, a premium-priced product in a commodity category, and the stubbornness to build distribution one grocer at a time.
