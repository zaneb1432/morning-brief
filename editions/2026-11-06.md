## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

*This is the first edition in the archive, so there are no prior founders to avoid. Future editions should check `editions/` and not repeat Walker.*

Sarah Breedlove was born on December 23, 1867, on a Louisiana plantation, the first child of her family born free after emancipation. Both parents died by the time she was seven. She worked as a laundress for decades, married young and was widowed early. By her thirties she was a single mother earning barely over a dollar a day, and she had a scalp ailment that was causing her hair to fall out.

She began selling products for Annie Turnbo Malone, then developed her own hair-care formula, which she called Wonderful Hair Grower. She moved to Denver in 1905, married Charles Joseph Walker in 1906 and took the name Madam C.J. Walker. Her business then moved to Pittsburgh (1908) and to Indianapolis (1910), where she set up headquarters and a factory.

Her real innovation was distribution. She trained a nationwide force of women agents, organized into Walker Clubs and later the Hair Culturists Union. Women with few other options earned independent incomes selling door to door. She put her profits into philanthropy and civil-rights causes, including anti-lynching efforts and the NAACP.

She died in 1919 at age 51, widely cited as one of America's first self-made female millionaires. Her company operated until 1981, and the brand was later revived and sold through Sephora.

Sources: [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/634630), [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/business-leaders/madam-cj-walker), [National Women's History Museum](https://www.womenshistory.org/node/160).
