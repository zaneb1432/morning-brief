## TECH & AI NEWS

**New York Becomes First U.S. State to Freeze Hyperscale Data Center Permits**

Governor Kathy Hochul signed an executive order on July 14 creating a one-year moratorium on new hyperscale data centers (defined as those using 50 megawatts or more of power), making New York the first state in the nation to halt this kind of AI infrastructure buildout. The pause is meant to buy time to develop a regulatory framework addressing grid strain, water use, land disruption, and noise pollution near proposed sites. It's a significant marker because New York's grid operator has roughly 25 data center proposals in its interconnection queue totaling about 9,340 megawatts of demand, with four hyperscale facilities already operating and 39 more applications pending. The move signals growing state-level pushback against the AI buildout's strain on local energy and water systems, and could become a template other states use as they weigh similar data center gold rushes against community and grid impacts.
Source: https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/first-statewide-moratorium-new-hyperscale-data-centers-launched-governor-kathy-hochul

**China's New AI "Companion" Rules Force ByteDance and Alibaba to Kill Humanlike Agent Features**

China's "Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic Interactive Services" took effect July 15, becoming the first national regulatory framework specifically targeting AI systems designed to simulate human personality and sustained emotional interaction. Ahead of the deadline, ByteDance's Doubao and Alibaba's Qwen both announced they are discontinuing their custom AI-agent creation features entirely, with all existing user-created companion agents ceasing to function; users have until October 15 to export their data before it becomes permanently inaccessible. The rules, issued jointly by the Cyberspace Administration of China and several ministries, require anti-addiction systems, identity verification for users under 14, and an always-available exit option, citing risks of extremist content, privacy leaks, and unhealthy dependence. It's notable both as a rare case of a major government moving faster than the West on a specific AI harm category, and because it forces two of China's biggest tech platforms to gut a consumer product line overnight rather than retrofit compliance — a sharp contrast to the more permissive approach Western companion-app makers currently operate under.
Source: https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3359482/bytedance-and-alibaba-disable-humanlike-ai-custom-agents-new-rules-loom

**Anthropic's "J-Space" Discovery Draws Scrutiny Over What It Actually Proves About Claude's Inner Workings**

Following Anthropic's early-July announcement that it had found a hidden internal region in Claude Opus 4.6 — dubbed "J-space" — using a new interpretability tool called the Jacobian lens (J-lens), MIT Technology Review published a follow-up analysis on July 13 examining what the discovery does and doesn't show. J-space is a small internal representation, holding only a few dozen concepts at a time, containing words related to what the model is likely to output in the near future — researchers draw a parallel to "global workspace theory" from neuroscience, where a few pieces of information get a "spotlight" while many specialized processes work in the background. Anthropic says monitoring this space could give it a new lever for understanding and steering model behavior before it manifests in outputs. The piece pushes back on some of the more sensational framing (comparisons to consciousness) circulating after the initial release, arguing the finding is a genuine interpretability advance but doesn't establish anything about subjective experience. This matters because interpretability — actually seeing what's happening inside frontier models rather than just observing outputs — is increasingly viewed as essential to AI safety, and claims in this area tend to get overhyped before independent scrutiny catches up.
Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/13/1140343/what-anthropics-latest-ai-discovery-does-and-doesnt-show/

**TSMC Posts All-Time Revenue Record as AI Chip Demand Breaks Seasonal Pattern**

TSMC reported June 2026 revenue of NT$442.68 billion, up 67.9% year-over-year, driving second-quarter sales above the company's own guidance and marking an all-time high. The jump is notable because it breaks a four-year pattern of seasonal summer declines in chip demand — evidence that AI-driven orders are now overriding normal industry cyclicality. TSMC's advanced 3-nanometer (N3) node and CoWoS advanced packaging capacity are both reportedly sold out through the end of the year, as every leading AI GPU and CPU maker competes for allocation. SemiAnalysis estimates TSMC is on pace to exceed $40 billion in AI-related chip revenue for 2026, roughly a quarter of its total projected revenue. As the manufacturing chokepoint underneath essentially every AI accelerator on the market (Nvidia, AMD, Google's TPUs, and custom silicon from Anthropic and OpenAI's partners all depend on TSMC capacity), this is a concrete signal that AI infrastructure spending is translating into real, sustained industrial demand rather than slowing down.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/13/tsmc-june-revenue-rises-percent-ahead-second-quarter.html

**Nvidia Cuts More Than Half Its Asian AI Chip Customers Amid Tightened Export Compliance**

Nvidia has more than halved the number of Asian customers authorized to buy its advanced AI chips after rolling out a stricter "whitelist" system with enhanced compliance reviews, according to Financial Times reporting on July 13-14. The crackdown focused on Singapore, Malaysia, and Japan, where Nvidia staff conducted on-site data center visits, contract verification, and end-user interviews — leading to the removal of numerous "neo-cloud" providers suspected of facilitating chip diversion to China. The move follows U.S. Commerce Department guidance issued in May aimed at closing loopholes that allowed advanced Blackwell processors to reach Chinese-linked entities through overseas subsidiaries despite export restrictions. This matters because it shows Nvidia proactively tightening its own distribution chain under government pressure rather than waiting for new legislation, and it underscores how much of the AI hardware supply chain now runs through geopolitically contested checkpoints — with real consequences for cloud providers in U.S.-aligned Asian markets who lose access even though they aren't in China themselves.
Source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nvidia-halves-asia-buyer-list-033140201.html

**IBM Shares Crash 25% in Worst Trading Day on Record as AI Capex Reshuffles Enterprise Spending**

IBM stock fell roughly 25% on July 14 — its worst single-day drop ever, surpassing even Black Monday 1987 — after the company issued a preliminary second-quarter warning showing adjusted EPS of $2.93 against a forecast of $3.02, and revenue of $17.2 billion versus an expected $17.86 billion. CEO Arvind Krishna attributed the shortfall specifically to enterprise clients redirecting quarterly capital spending away from IBM's software and infrastructure businesses and toward servers, storage, and memory purchases, racing to secure supply-constrained hardware ahead of anticipated price increases. Trading volume hit roughly 551% of IBM's three-month average as investors repriced the stock. The story is significant beyond IBM itself: it's a concrete data point showing how the AI buildout is actively cannibalizing adjacent enterprise IT spending, forcing even large, diversified tech vendors to absorb collateral damage from the industry-wide scramble for AI-related hardware.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/14/ibm-warns-second-quarter-earnings-fell-short-of-expectations.html

## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY

### Chuck Feeney: The Duty-Free Billionaire Who Gave It All Away — In Secret

Chuck Feeney made his fortune selling perfume, liquor, and cigarettes to sailors and tourists — then spent four decades trying to give every dollar of it away before he died, without anyone finding out.

Feeney grew up working-class in Elizabeth, New Jersey, the son of an insurance underwriter and a nurse. After a stint as a U.S. Air Force radio operator during the Korean War, he used the GI Bill to attend Cornell's hotel school, where classmates nicknamed him "the sandwich man" for hawking bologna sandwiches to hungry students between classes — an early sign of the hustle that would define him. At Cornell he met Robert Miller, a fellow student who would become his business partner. Years later, broke and drifting around the south of France after running a summer camp for U.S. sailors, Feeney bumped into Miller in Barcelona. He had a simple idea: American servicemen stationed overseas were desperate for name-brand liquor and perfume they couldn't easily get, and if you sold it to them tax-free, they'd buy it by the case. On November 7, 1960, the two founded what became Duty Free Shoppers (DFS), opening outlets in Hong Kong and Honolulu and eventually winning the concession at Hong Kong's Kai Tak Airport. DFS rode the explosion of postwar Japanese overseas travel, and by the 1980s it was generating enormous profits from tourists buying Chanel and Dunhill on their way through Pacific airport terminals.

Here is where Feeney's story stops looking like an ordinary rags-to-riches tale. In 1984, at the height of his wealth, he quietly transferred his entire 38.75% stake in DFS — then worth roughly $500 million — into a foundation he'd created two years earlier called Atlantic Philanthropies. He told almost no one, not even his business partners, who spent years assuming Feeney was still a fellow billionaire owner while he had, in fact, given nearly everything away. He lived like it too: flying economy class well into his 80s, wearing a $10 Casio watch, carrying papers in a plastic bag instead of a briefcase, and never owning a house or a car. He called his philosophy "Giving While Living" — the conviction that money does more good deployed aggressively during a donor's lifetime than parceled out by a foundation for a century after death. Atlantic Philanthropies went on to fund the end of apartheid-era institutions in South Africa, peace efforts in Northern Ireland, public health initiatives worldwide, and biomedical research buildings at Cornell, UCSF, and elsewhere — often given anonymously, with Feeney's name deliberately kept off buildings he'd funded.

His secrecy finally broke in 1997 during a legal dispute over the sale of DFS to LVMH, which forced disclosure of the foundation's ownership. The revelation stunned Wall Street and inspired a wave of "giving pledge" style philanthropy — Bill Gates and Warren Buffett both later credited Feeney as a model. In September 2020, Atlantic Philanthropies formally closed its doors, its mission complete: more than $8 billion given away, essentially all of it, by design. Feeney died in October 2023 at age 92, having spent his final years living in a modest rented apartment in San Francisco. He wasn't chasing a legacy of monuments — he was trying to prove that dying broke, on purpose, after a lifetime of giving, could be its own kind of achievement.
