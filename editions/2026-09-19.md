## TECH & AI NEWS

**OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind are quietly building a shared AI "standards body."** A working group of executives from the three labs, one rung below CEO level, has been meeting regularly since July to design a self-regulatory body modeled on FINRA, the organization that oversees US brokerages. The idea originated with Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis's proposal for an independent group that would test frontier models before release — initially on a voluntary basis, with labs sharing models up to 30 days ahead of launch, before formalizing into a requirement to pass muster before US deployment. OpenAI's chief global affairs officer, Chris Lehane, confirmed to Reuters and Bloomberg that talks have been underway "for several weeks" and said the company doesn't believe it needs an antitrust waiver to proceed. The effort matters because it's the clearest sign yet that the frontier labs would rather write their own safety rulebook than wait for Washington to write one for them — a bet that self-regulation can pre-empt statute, the same playbook the finance industry ran with FINRA nearly a century ago. [Coverage via TechRound](https://techround.co.uk/news/openai-anthropic-and-google-are-discreetly-building-their-own-ai-standards-body-what-would-that-actually-decide/) · [Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/news-anthropic-openai-google-ai-standards-body/)

**The US House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417–3, forcing AI data centers to pay the full cost of the grid upgrades they trigger.** The bipartisan bill, passed Wednesday, encourages state utility regulators to make large computational loads — anything over 100 MW — cover the complete additional cost of generation, transmission, and distribution capacity their facilities demand, rather than spreading that cost across ordinary ratepayers. It's a direct response to a growing voter backlash over electricity bills rising in tandem with the AI buildout. The bill's near-unanimous passage (only Reps. Summer Lee, Delia Ramirez, and Rashida Tlaib voted no) shows how bipartisan the "make Big Tech pay for its own power" sentiment has become — but a Senate GOP attempt to fast-track a companion bill failed the very next day amid Democratic objections that it relies on voluntary state commitments rather than a hard mandate, so the fight over enforcement teeth is far from over. [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/house-passes-ratepayer-protection-bill-data-centers/830658/) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/ai-data-center-utility-cost-senate.html)

**Dario Amodei's call to keep choking off China's chip supply drew a sharp rebuke from Beijing, days ahead of a Trump-Xi summit.** In a September 12 essay titled "We Must Pace the Frontier," the Anthropic CEO argued that a Chinese lead in AI "would pose grave danger for the United States and the world" and urged Washington to maintain restrictions on advanced AI chips and chipmaking equipment sold to China. China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun dismissed the essay as "fear-mongering" on September 14, and state-run Global Times called it a thinly veiled containment strategy. The timing is pointed: the rebuke landed just two days before senior US and Chinese officials were due to hold preparatory AI-governance talks ahead of an expected Trump-Xi meeting on September 24, meaning chip export policy is now explicitly on the table for a head-of-state summit. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/14/nx-s1-5968456/china-hits-back-ai-development) · [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/china-foreign-ministry-rebukes-amodei-essay-as-fear-mongering/)

**Z.ai claims it served a 320-billion-parameter frontier model entirely on domestic Chinese chips — the first claimed frontier-scale run on Chinese silicon.** The company revealed on August 26 that "Ox Alpha," a model it had been anonymously testing on OpenRouter since August 20, was actually GLM-5.3-Flash — a 320B-parameter, 18B-active hybrid model, the first multimodal entry in its GLM-5 line. Z.ai founder Jie Tang says the model's public preview ran on roughly 100,000 domestic accelerators via a custom inference engine built on SGLang, at a per-token cost the company says is comparable to mainstream Nvidia GPUs. The catch: Z.ai named no chip vendor and published no independently verifiable throughput or power figures — CNBC said it could not confirm the claim. If even partly true, though, it's a meaningful data point in the US-China chip race precisely as export-control policy is being litigated in public between Amodei and Beijing. [Implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/zai-glm-5-3-flash-chinese-chips-nvidia-cost/) · [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/z-ai-details-glm-5-3-flash-inference-build-on-100-000-chinese-chips/)

**Anthropic's fourth threat intelligence report documents AI moving from "assisting" attacks to "running" them.** Published September 10 and covering disrupted activity from December 2025 through August 2026 across roughly 40 tracked threat groups, the report spans seven harm categories: cyber operations, influence campaigns, surveillance, fraud, biological misuse, conventional weapons development, and model distillation. Highlights include militants in Yemen attempting to use Claude's coding tools to develop rocket guidance software, Iran-linked actors targeting US Navy vessels, a Russia-linked espionage campaign that used Claude to automate reconnaissance and rebuild tooling after detection against more than 20 organizations, and state-linked distillation campaigns out of China attempting to copy Claude's capabilities. Anthropic says it disrupted every operation described. It's a notable escalation in how frontier labs are documenting nation-state and criminal misuse of their own products — and a preview of the kind of evidence that will likely shape the standards-body and export-control debates above. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)

## STARTUP WORLD

**Positron AI raised $875 million at a $5 billion valuation to build memory-heavy chips that undercut Nvidia on inference.** The Reno-based startup, founded in 2023, closed an oversubscribed Series C (a $375M tranche plus a $500M "C-1") co-led by NEA, Atreides Management, Valor Equity Partners, Andra Capital, SemiAnalysis Capital, and Jim Clark — the founder of Silicon Graphics and Netscape. The money funds the tapeout of its next chip, Asimov, which pairs Positron's compute architecture with up to 2,304 GB of commodity LPDDR5X memory per chip, plus a 2MW+ engineering data center and Positron's "Titan" inference system. Its first chip, Atlas, was already fabbed by Intel in the US and is shipping to customers — meaning this isn't a paper roadmap but a company already generating inference revenue against Nvidia. [DataCenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/positron-ai-raises-875m-at-a-5bn-valuation-to-support-develop-of-next-generation-hardware/) · [Investing.com/Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/ai-chip-startup-positrons-valuation-skyrockets-in-latest-funding-round-4896462)

**Temporal closed a $550 million Series E at a $12.55 billion valuation as "durable execution" infrastructure becomes AI-agent plumbing.** The September 14 round was co-led by Lightspeed, Wellington Management, Goldman Sachs Alternatives' growth-equity arm, and Tiger Global, with T. Rowe Price and SV Angel joining and existing backers a16z, Sequoia, Index, GIC, Sapphire, and Amplify all returning. The numbers explain the enthusiasm: Temporal's annualized revenue run rate has crossed $250 million (up more than 200% year-over-year), net dollar retention has stayed above 200% since February, and its managed cloud processed 1.9 trillion actions in August alone — up 350% year-over-year. Customers now include OpenAI, Snap, Nvidia, Netflix, and JPMorgan Chase, underscoring that as companies wire up autonomous AI agents, they need boring, reliable workflow orchestration underneath them — and that's turned into one of the fastest-growing infrastructure businesses in the market. [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/temporal-raises-550m-hits-12-55b-valuation-as-agentic-ai-wave-fuels-massive-growth/) · [Temporal blog](https://temporal.io/blog/temporal-raises-usd550m-series-e-at-usd12-55b-valuation-ai)

**Pixxel raised $100 million — India's largest-ever space-tech round — to expand from imaging satellites into orbital data centers.** The Bangalore-based company's Series C was co-led by Temasek and Seraphim Space, with new investors 360 ONE Asset and IMM Investment joining returning backers Radical Ventures and growX. Total funding now stands at $195 million. Pixxel is broadening beyond its hyperspectral-imaging roots into what it calls "planetary infrastructure" — sensing satellites, Earth-intelligence software, sovereign space systems, satellite manufacturing, and a demonstration satellite for orbital data centers — while also leading a 12-satellite public-private Earth observation constellation for the Indian government under IN-SPACe. It's a reminder that the AI infrastructure buildout is starting to reach past terrestrial data centers into orbit. [Pixxel](https://www.pixxel.space/news/pixxel-raises-100-million-in-series-c-funding-to-build-the-infrastructure-for-a-changing-planet) · [TechCrunch/TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/pixxel-100m-series-c-temasek-seraphim)

**Infillion is acquiring Foursquare, merging 16 billion location check-ins into an ad-measurement platform.** Announced September 18 as an Axios exclusive, the deal brings Foursquare's real-world visitation data — more than 100 million points of interest across 200 countries and aggregated coverage of 250 million US devices — into Infillion's platform alongside its existing Catalina purchase-intelligence data. The pitch is a rare "closed loop": advertisers can now trace an ad impression through to a real-world store visit and a real-world purchase on one platform. Foursquare will keep operating as an independent brand and continue selling data to Infillion's competitors, and the deal is expected to grow Infillion's revenue and headcount by 20–25%. Terms weren't disclosed. It's a sign that even in an AI-obsessed funding market, old-guard location data still has real strategic value to the right buyer. [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/18/infillion-acquires-foursquare-location-data) · [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/marketers/infillion-acquires-foursquare-adding-more-location-data-to-its-ever-growing-ad-tech-stack/)

**Relay, a five-year-old Zapier alternative, shut down entirely this week — and its founder landed at Google.** Relay stopped serving free users on August 15 and cut off paying customers on September 14, closing out a company that had raised $8.1 million across a Khosla-led seed and an a16z-led follow-on. The proximate cause: as OpenAI, Google, and other platforms built agent-based workflow automation directly into their own products, a standalone automation layer lost its reason to exist. Founder and CEO Jacob Bank is now rejoining Google as VP of Product for Chrome, leading product and developer relations for the browser — a clean, soft-landing acquihire-by-attrition that TechCrunch has been cataloguing as part of a running "AI graveyard" of 2026 shutdowns. It's a small but sharp illustration of the current startup risk in building anything that a frontier lab can casually absorb as a feature. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/ai-automation-startup-relay-shuts-down-staff-joins-googles-chrome-team/) · [TechCrunch "AI graveyard"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/the-ai-graveyard-a-running-list-of-projects-and-startups-that-didnt-make-it/)

## VENTURE CAPITAL

**Bain Capital Ventures closed a $1.6 billion fund — its 11th — betting the next winners will be the infrastructure layer underneath AGI, not AGI itself.** Announced September 16–17, the fund is 14% larger than BCV's prior $1.4 billion vehicle from three years ago and will back 30–40 companies from seed through Series B. BCV's stated thesis, notably, is that AGI has effectively already arrived, and that the next wave of venture-scale outcomes will come from infrastructure, healthcare, physical AI, and security companies that make that intelligence usable and efficient — not from chasing another foundation-model bet. The firm is also leaning on its affiliation with Bain Capital's credit, real estate, and private-equity arms to offer founders debt facilities and infrastructure partnerships alongside equity, a structural edge pure-play VCs can't easily match. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/how-bain-capital-ventures-plans-to-deploy-its-fresh-1-6b-fund/) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/bain-capital-ventures-raises-1-6-billion-for-life-after-agi)

**The investor syndicate behind Positron's $875 million round is itself a story: a market-research firm and a legendary founder are now writing venture checks.** SemiAnalysis Capital — the investing arm spun out of the well-known chip-analysis newsletter SemiAnalysis — co-led alongside NEA, Atreides, Valor Equity, and Andra Capital, with Jim Clark (Silicon Graphics, Netscape) investing personally rather than through a fund. Analysts-turned-investors and legacy founder angels stepping into a $5 billion round signals how much specialized, technical credibility now matters in chip-infrastructure deals — generalist growth capital alone isn't winning these rounds anymore. [DataCenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/positron-ai-raises-875m-at-a-5bn-valuation-to-support-develop-of-next-generation-hardware/)

**A widely-cited Commonfund research note argues AI is making venture returns more winner-take-all than at any point in the asset class's history.** The piece finds that the top 1% of venture-backed companies now account for roughly 80% of total exit value since 2023 (45% even excluding SpaceX's IPO) — up from just 17% in 2005–2010 and 34% in 2017–2022 — while the five largest VC firms captured about 73% of all new fund commitments in Q1 2026 alone. It's not breaking news (published in July), but it's the analytical backdrop against which every mega-round above should be read: capital concentration among both the biggest funds and the biggest portfolio outcomes is now a defining, self-reinforcing feature of AI-era venture capital, not a temporary side effect of the boom. [Commonfund](https://www.commonfund.org/cf-private-equity/ai-is-steepening-the-power-law-in-venture-capital)

## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY: Lynn Conway

Before there was a "fabless" chip industry — before a startup could design a processor without owning a fab, before Positron or any other chip company in today's brief could exist — there was Lynn Conway, quietly rewriting the rules of how chips get designed while living, by necessity, a double life.

Conway was born in 1938 in Mount Vernon, New York, and showed an early gift for math and science. She entered MIT in 1955, but in 1959 she left the school while struggling with gender dysphoria and an attempted transition that the era offered her no safe way to complete. She picked herself back up, earning a bachelor's and a master's in electrical engineering from Columbia in 1962 and 1963, and in 1964 was recruited to IBM Research, where she joined a team building an advanced supercomputer architecture. There she invented multiple-issue, out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling — the technique, still called "superscalar" execution, that lets a processor run several instructions at once out of their original order for speed. It's a foundational idea baked into essentially every modern CPU.

In 1968, IBM fired her — after she told the company of her intention to transition. She lost her job, her name, and, for three decades, credit for her own invention. Conway rebuilt her entire professional life from zero under a new identity: a contract programmer at Computer Applications Inc., then a rapid rise to computer architect at Memorex. In 1973 she joined Xerox PARC, the legendary research lab that also produced the mouse and the graphical user interface. There, working with Caltech professor Carver Mead starting around 1978, she co-created what's now known as the Mead-Conway VLSI revolution: a set of scalable, standardized design rules that let engineers design complex chips the way software engineers write modular code, radically simplifying what had been an artisanal, fab-specific craft. Their textbook, *Introduction to VLSI Systems*, became the field's bible, and Conway personally pioneered teaching the new methodology, including a visiting stint at MIT in 1978. She and Mead also built MOSIS, an early internet-based service that let universities and small companies get prototype chips fabricated cheaply and quickly — the direct ancestor of the entire fabless-design ecosystem that underpins the chip industry, and the chip startups, of 2026.

Conway went on to DARPA in the early 1980s to help run the Strategic Computing Initiative, then joined the University of Michigan faculty in 1985, retiring as professor emerita in 1998. Throughout all of it, her IBM history and her gender history stayed hidden — until 1999, when she learned that old records might surface publicly and chose to come out to friends and colleagues herself rather than be outed. In November 2000, journalist Michael Hiltzik profiled her life in the Los Angeles Times Magazine under the headline "Through the Gender Labyrinth," and Scientific American followed with its own feature the same year. Rather than retreat from the exposure, Conway became one of the most visible transgender advocates in American public life, running a widely used personal website supporting other trans women and speaking publicly for two more decades.

IBM formally apologized to her in 2020 — 52 years after firing her — for the way she was treated. By then she'd already collected the field's highest honors: election to the National Academy of Engineering (1989), the Computer Pioneer Award (2009), Computer History Museum Fellow (2014), the IEEE/RSE James Clerk Maxwell Medal (2015), and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame (2023). She died on June 9, 2024, at age 86.

Conway's story is really two intertwined ones: an engineer whose two separate inventions — one in computer architecture, one in chip design methodology — each independently reshaped the industry; and a woman forced to erase and rebuild her identity to survive in that same industry, who ultimately outlived the shame that was imposed on her and turned her story into an advocacy platform instead. Every fabless chip startup raising a mega-round this week is, whether it knows it or not, working inside a design paradigm she built.
