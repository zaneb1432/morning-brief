## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

*(Written from background knowledge, not this session's searches. No earlier editions exist in the repo.)*

Sarah Breedlove was born on a Louisiana cotton plantation in 1867, the first of her family born free after emancipation. She was orphaned at seven. She married at 14 and was widowed at about 20 with a young daughter, A'Lelia. She worked for years as a washerwoman in St. Louis, earning around a dollar a day. In her late thirties she began losing her hair to scalp disease, which was common among women who rarely bathed and who used harsh lye soaps and hot irons.

After experimenting with remedies, including products from Annie Malone's Poro company, she developed her own hair-growth treatment. She moved to Denver, married the newspaper salesman Charles J. Walker, and in 1906 launched "Madam C.J. Walker's Wonderful Hair Grower". She sold door to door and gave demonstrations in Black churches and homes.

Her real innovation was distribution. She trained thousands of Black women as "beauty culturists" and paid them commissions. That gave them a path out of domestic labor and into independent income. She founded Lelia College to train agents and later built a factory in Indianapolis. She also organized her agents into the National Beauty Culturists and Benevolent Association, an early union-like network.

She funded the NAACP's anti-lynching efforts, gave to the YMCA and to Tuskegee, and spoke publicly about economic independence. She died in 1919, and she is widely credited as one of the first self-made American women millionaires. Her business model is still instructive: she built the sales channel and the people network, not just the product.
