## Tech & AI News

### OpenAI Launches GPT-5.6 Family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — After Brief Government-Mandated Hold
OpenAI publicly rolled out its GPT-5.6 model family on July 9, roughly two weeks after the White House had asked the company to limit early access to a small group of trusted partners. The lineup includes three tiers: Sol, the flagship model built for frontier reasoning and long-horizon agentic work; Terra, a balanced everyday model priced at half the cost of GPT-5.5 with comparable performance; and Luna, the fastest and most affordable option. Pricing undercuts rivals — Sol runs $5/$30 per million input/output tokens, Terra $2.50/$15, and Luna $1/$6 — while OpenAI also introduced "ultra," a mode that coordinates multiple agents in parallel to finish complex tasks faster. The models are rolling out across ChatGPT, Codex, and the API, with improved capabilities in coding, biology, and cybersecurity. The release intensifies the pricing and capability war with Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5/Fable 5 and Google's delayed Gemini 3.5 Pro, and signals that frontier labs are now competing as much on cost-efficiency as raw benchmark scores.
Source: https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/

### Apple Sues OpenAI, Alleging Coordinated Theft of Trade Secrets for Hardware Products
Apple filed a lawsuit against OpenAI in federal court in Northern California on July 10, alleging that OpenAI engaged in trade-secret theft "at every level" — from technical staff up to its Chief Hardware Officer — to accelerate development of its own consumer AI hardware. The complaint names OpenAI hardware chief Tang Tan, a former Apple VP, who allegedly directed job candidates still employed at Apple to bring "actual parts" to interviews for "show and tell" sessions, and former Apple employee Chang Liu, accused of stealing a company laptop and downloading dozens of confidential files on unreleased products and engineering specs. Apple says more than 400 former employees now work at OpenAI. The suit marks a dramatic reversal for two companies that partnered in 2024 to integrate ChatGPT into iOS, and it escalates into open legal warfare just as OpenAI is reportedly preparing its own hardware devices. OpenAI has denied the allegations, saying it has "no interest in other companies' trade secrets."
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/10/apple-openai-lawsuit-trade-secrets.html

### SK Hynix Debuts on Nasdaq in Record $26.5 Billion Listing, Stock Jumps 13%
SK Hynix, the world's second-largest memory chipmaker and a key supplier of high-bandwidth memory for Nvidia's AI chips, began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker SKHY on July 10 in one of the largest corporate ADR offerings in Wall Street history. Shares priced at $149 and jumped 13% on debut to close near $168, raising roughly $26.5 billion (with the offering sized up to $29 billion) for capacity expansion. The company's chairman told CNBC that "demand is enormous," reflecting how the AI boom's insatiable appetite for memory chips is reshaping global capital markets. The listing is widely seen as an effort to close the long-standing valuation gap between SK Hynix and its American peers like Micron by giving international AI-focused investors direct access. It underscores how memory and chip supply — not just model releases — have become central battlegrounds in the AI infrastructure race.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/10/sk-hynix-skhy-stock-nasdaq.html

### Meta to Begin Production of In-House "Iris" AI Chip in September, Targeting 14 Gigawatts of Compute
An internal Meta memo reported on July 9 revealed the company plans to move its next-generation custom AI chip, code-named Iris, into production in September as part of a push to roughly double its computing capacity to 14 gigawatts next year. Iris is the latest generation of Meta's Training and Inference Accelerator (MTIA) program, co-designed with Broadcom and manufactured by TSMC; the chip reportedly passed six weeks of testing with no major issues. Meta intends to launch a new chip roughly every six months through 2027 — far faster than the industry's typical annual cadence — aiming to cut reliance on costly third-party GPUs and lower the energy and financial burden of running AI across Facebook and Instagram. The move places Meta alongside Google and Amazon in the race to vertically integrate custom silicon, intensifying competitive pressure on Nvidia even as its Blackwell chips remain supply-constrained.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/metas-new-ai-chips-will-begin-production-in-september/

### SpaceXAI Releases Grok 4.5, Its First Model Since Going Public and Acquiring Cursor
SpaceXAI (Elon Musk's xAI, now under a merged brand) launched Grok 4.5 on July 8, describing it as an "Opus-class" model that is faster and more token-efficient than rivals while undercutting them on price — $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, versus Anthropic's Opus 4.8 at $5/$25. It is the company's first release since going public and acquiring AI coding startup Cursor, and it is being positioned primarily as a coding and agentic-work tool rather than a consumer chatbot, trained alongside Cursor to improve engineering and knowledge-work performance. The model rolled out in Grok Build, within Cursor across all plans, and via the SpaceXAI API console, though it remains unavailable in the EU pending expected mid-July access. Musk has also touted the model as "maximally truth-seeking" relative to competitors. The release, alongside GPT-5.6 and the anticipated Gemini 3.5 Pro, marks a compressed few days of frontier-model launches that show the AI lab arms race accelerating on both capability and cost.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/spacexai-releases-grok-4-5-which-elon-describes-as-an-opus-class-model/

## Startup World

### Prime Intellect Raises $130M Series A to Help Enterprises Build Their Own AI Agents
Prime Intellect announced a $130 million Series A on July 8 at a $1 billion valuation, led by Radical Ventures with participation from Nvidia Ventures, Intel Capital, Dell Technologies Capital, and Iconiq, plus angel investors including Perplexity's Aravind Srinivas, Box's Aaron Levie, and Harvey's Winston Weinberg. The company, led by co-founder and CEO Vincent Weisser and founded in 2024, has already reached roughly $100 million in annual recurring revenue by helping enterprises build and own their AI agents rather than renting capability from the frontier labs. The round's investor list — spanning both chipmakers and enterprise-software VCs — signals how infrastructure plays adjacent to the big model labs are attracting serious capital of their own. It's also a reminder that "agent infrastructure" has become one of the most competitive sub-categories in AI venture right now, sitting between raw model access and finished enterprise products.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/prime-intellect-raises-130m-series-a-to-help-enterprises-build-their-own-ai-agents/

### SambaNova Lands $1B First Close at $11B Valuation
SambaNova, the Intel-backed AI chip and inference startup, announced on July 8 the first close of a $1 billion financing round led by General Atlantic at an $11 billion valuation. The raise positions SambaNova as one of the best-funded challengers to Nvidia in the AI inference hardware market, at a moment when chip supply — not just model quality — is a binding constraint on how fast AI companies can scale. The scale of the round, and General Atlantic's lead role, also shows growth-stage private equity firms increasingly competing directly with traditional venture funds for the biggest AI infrastructure deals. More of the financing is expected to close in subsequent tranches.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/08/sambanova-ai-chip-funding-valuation.html

### Paris's Gradium Raises $100M Seed Extension, Backed by Nvidia
Gradium, a Paris-based voice-AI startup spun out of French AI lab Kyutai and founded by Neil Zeghidour and colleagues, extended its seed round by $100 million on July 9, with new backing from Nvidia. The company had originally raised a $70 million seed round in December, meaning it has now brought in roughly $170 million before even reaching a Series A — an unusually large seed stack that reflects how much capital is chasing voice-AI specifically as a category distinct from general-purpose chatbots. Nvidia's participation as a strategic investor also fits a broader pattern of the chipmaker taking equity stakes across the AI startup ecosystem it supplies, giving it visibility (and influence) deep into emerging application layers.

### Ollama Raises $65M Series B as Open-Model Usage Nearly Doubles
Ollama, the open-source tool that lets developers run open-weight AI models locally and scale to the cloud, raised a $65 million Series B on July 9 led by Theory Ventures, with Benchmark, 8VC, Y Combinator, Pace Capital, 49 Palms, and GTMFund also participating — bringing total funding to $88 million. Founded by Jeffrey Morgan and Michael Chiang, the company has seen its developer base nearly double since January to 8.9 million users, adding almost a million new installs a week, and says it's now used within 85% of the Fortune 500, including regulated industries like government, healthcare, and finance. The round is a useful data point on how much real developer demand exists for open-weight models and local-first tooling, even as headline funding dollars concentrate in a handful of closed-model frontier labs.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/popular-open-source-ai-developer-tool-ollama-raises-65m-grows-to-nearly-9m-users/

### Nigerian Fintech Gigbanc Winds Down Operations

## Venture Capital

### Paradigm Closes $1.2 Billion Fourth Fund, Pushes Crypto VC Into AI and Robotics
Crypto-focused venture firm Paradigm announced on July 8 that it closed its fourth fund at $1.2 billion, a notable widening of its mandate beyond digital assets into artificial intelligence, robotics, and other frontier technologies while keeping crypto at the core of its strategy. The new fund fell slightly short of the $1.5 billion the firm was originally targeting, but it still pushes Paradigm's total assets under management to roughly $11.9 billion, alongside its $850 million early-stage crypto fund raised in 2024. Founded in 2018 by former Sequoia partner Matt Huang and Coinbase co-founder Fred Ehrsam, Paradigm has already deployed capital from the new vehicle into companies like drone-delivery firm Zipline and orbital-defense startup True Anomaly. The raise signals that even crypto-native funds are reallocating toward the AI and hard-tech themes dominating 2026 venture activity, rather than treating them as separate categories.
Source: https://techfundingnews.com/crypto-vc-paradigm-just-closeds-1-2b-fourth-fund-to-chase-ai-and-robotics/

### B Capital Closes Oversubscribed $500 Million Early-Stage AI Fund at Hard Cap
B Capital, the multi-stage firm with over $12 billion in AUM, announced on July 6 the final close of its Ascent Fund III at a $500 million hard cap, nearly double the size of its $254 million predecessor fund closed in 2022. The fund was oversubscribed with strong support from both existing and new limited partners, and will invest primarily at Seed through Series B in companies spanning AI infrastructure, robotics, healthcare, and energy, with a focus on hubs in North America and Asia. Ascent Fund III has already backed more than 20 companies, including Apptronik, Havoc AI, and Star Catcher, several of which have gone on to raise substantial follow-on rounds — a sign of continued LP appetite for early-stage AI-adjacent bets even as later-stage mega-rounds dominate the headlines.

### BAE Systems-Backed Expeditions Closes €197M Fund for European Defense Startups
Expeditions, a defense-tech venture firm backed by BAE Systems and the NATO Innovation Fund, announced on July 7 that it closed its Fund II at €197 million (about $225 million), overshooting an earlier €150 million target thanks in part to a €25 million check from BAE. The fund will back as many as 40 early-stage European startups building technologies with immediate operational relevance to the continent's defense capability gaps, spanning cybersecurity, autonomy, AI, quantum computing, and space. The raise comes as European defense and security startups pulled in a record $8.7 billion in 2025 — nearly double the prior year — as the continent seeks to build and control its own military technology after capability gaps were exposed by the war in Ukraine. The LP base includes Poland's state-backed Polish Development Fund and early executives from Skype, Wise, Bolt, and Snowflake, underscoring how sovereign-tech investing is drawing both institutional and operator capital.

## Founder Spotlight: Momofuku Ando

Most founder stories start young. Momofuku Ando's real story starts at 47, standing in the wreckage of his second bankruptcy, wondering what he had left to lose.

Ando was born in 1910 in Chiayi, Taiwan, then a Japanese colony, and orphaned as a small child. Raised by his grandparents, who ran a textile shop, he grew up with merchant instincts and little patience for sitting still. By 22 he had moved to Japan and started his own textile company. Over the following two decades he built and lost fortunes with a regularity that would have broken most people: he spent two years in prison after the war on a tax-related charge tied to a scholarship fund he'd financed, then rebuilt again, only to watch a company he'd founded collapse in a chain-reaction bankruptcy when a firm he'd guaranteed loans for went under. He started over yet again, this time in Ikeda, a small city near Osaka, running a modest company that made salt.

Then, in 1957, the credit union where Ando kept his savings failed. He lost nearly everything a second time, in his late forties, with a family to support and no company left to his name.

It's what happened next that made him a legend. Rather than take a job or start another conventional business, Ando fixated on a problem he'd noticed in postwar Japan: food was scarce, and people were lining up for hours in the cold for a bowl of ramen from black-market street stalls. He became convinced that if he could make noodles that were tasty, cheap, and could be prepared in minutes with nothing but hot water, he could feed a hungry, exhausted nation.

He built a small wooden shed in his backyard, and for the better part of a year he worked alone, sleeping around four hours a night, running crude experiments with a rented noodle-making machine. His breakthrough came, as the story is told, while watching his wife make tempura: he noticed the hot oil instantly drove the moisture out of the batter, leaving it porous and crisp. He reasoned that if he flash-fried pre-cooked, pre-seasoned noodles the same way, the tiny holes left behind would let boiling water rehydrate them almost instantly. On August 25, 1958, at age 48, he launched "Chikin Ramen" — the world's first commercial instant noodle — under a company that would become Nissin Foods. It sold at roughly six times the price of fresh noodles and was treated as a luxury item, but it caught on fast.

Ando kept innovating well past the age most founders retire. In 1966, on a trip to the United States, he watched American supermarket buyers break his noodles in half, drop them into paper cups, and pour hot water over them because they had no bowls or chopsticks handy. Rather than dismiss the workaround, he redesigned the product around it: in 1971, at 61, he launched Cup Noodles in a foam cup, engineered with a narrower base to keep the noodles submerged — a packaging innovation that turned instant ramen into a genuinely global product. Decades later, at 94, he was still at it, developing "Space Ram," a vacuum-sealed noodle dish that flew with Japanese astronaut Soichi Noguchi aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery in 2005.

Ando died in January 2007 at 96. Nissin now sells over 100 billion servings of instant noodles a year worldwide.

What makes Ando's story worth telling isn't just the invention — it's the sequence. He didn't found his defining company as a young man with nothing to lose; he found it after losing everything twice, at an age when reinvention is supposed to be over. And even after he'd made it, he kept treating small, unglamorous observations — a wife frying tempura, shoppers improvising with paper cups — as product roadmaps. Persistence past failure is a common enough newsletter moral. Persistence past success, still tinkering with your product at 94, is the rarer and more useful lesson.
