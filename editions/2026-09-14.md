## 1. TECH & AI NEWS

**Anthropic's Amodei calls for an industry-wide AI slowdown — and Altman agrees.** In a roughly 3,800-word essay published Saturday, September 12, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei argued that frontier AI development is moving faster than researchers' ability to understand or control it, and warned that swarms of autonomous, poorly-supervised AI agents could pose serious risks to the internet within as little as six months if left unchecked. He laid out a three-step plan: Anthropic has "unilaterally" committed to giving third-party evaluators employee-level access to verify its safety practices; he wants leading AI labs in democratic countries to coordinate on common safety standards; and, longer-term, he wants democratic and authoritarian governments to coordinate with each other. The essay landed just days after an Anthropic employee reportedly resigned over safety concerns. Within hours, OpenAI's Sam Altman publicly endorsed the idea of slowing the pace of frontier-model releases and said OpenAI would adopt one of Amodei's proposed safeguards, while Microsoft's Satya Nadella said the company "welcomes" more deliberate pacing and unveiled a new AI Code of Conduct. This is a notable reversal for an industry that has spent the last three years racing on release cadence, and it matters because it's the first time competing lab CEOs have publicly synchronized around "slow down" rather than "ship faster." Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/13/china-dilemma-ai-slowdown-anthropic.html), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/12/anthropic-ai-amodei-pacing), [Slashdot](https://slashdot.org/story/26/09/12/1738240/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-calls-for-ai-slowdown)

**OpenAI shelves its 2026 IPO as Anthropic presses toward a record $2 trillion listing.** Sam Altman told Fortune this week that going public "right now would be an ill-advised moment," ruling out a 2026 listing and declining to commit to 2027 either, citing unresolved safety and alignment work and the need for government and industry to align first. OpenAI had confidentially filed S-1 paperwork with the SEC back in June and had been weighing a sub-$1 trillion valuation this year versus waiting for a bigger number later. The timing is striking because rival Anthropic — which surpassed OpenAI in private valuation this spring at $965 billion — is reportedly still pointing toward an October 2026 IPO that could value it at up to $2 trillion, based on projected 2028 revenue of $190–200 billion and a current annualized revenue run rate north of $65 billion. If it happens, it would be the largest IPO in history, eclipsing SpaceX's $1.77 trillion debut earlier this year. The contrast — Anthropic's CEO calling publicly for the industry to slow down while his own company simultaneously chases a record-breaking public listing — is drawing plenty of commentary. Source: [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/09/61754670/openai-ipo-delay-safety-alignment-ai), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/2026/openai-pauses-ipo-plans-amid-ai-safety-worries/), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/ai-trade-hits-a-wall-amid-report-that-openai-will-delay-ipo-until-2027-150642366.html)

**Washington scrambles for AI guardrails as its legislative window closes.** With House Speaker Mike Johnson sending members home after this week until after November's midterms, Congress has only days to act on AI regulation even as the CEOs of Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI have all, in the span of a week, publicly called for some form of industry regulation or slowdown. In the absence of federal action, states continue to fill the gap — Connecticut's SB 5 passed this year as the most comprehensive state AI law yet, creating a regulatory sandbox, chatbot controls, and a study of independent AI verification bodies, while the EU AI Act continues to set the pace globally. The story matters because it captures a rare moment of industry-regulator role reversal: the labs are now asking for rules faster than the government is able to write them. Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/13/ai-congress-anthropic-openai-crisis.html), [Townhall](https://townhall.com/news/josephchalfant/2026/09/13/ai-leaders-call-for-regulation-of-the-industry-n2682890)

**Salesforce gives Agentforce seven named, job-titled AI employees ahead of Dreamforce.** On September 11, Salesforce introduced seven purpose-built Agentforce agents — Casey (customer service), Paige (IT/HR), Carter (commerce), Hunter (outbound sales), Marshall (supply chain), Piper (inbound pipeline) and Fin (customer experience) — each sitting on top of the company's existing Customer 360 data, permissions and business rules. Most are generally available now; Hunter, built on a new "long-horizon" runtime that can autonomously run a sales pipeline for weeks rather than a single chat session, is in pilot with GA targeted for November. Salesforce also introduced a "Trusted Enterprise AI Harness" governance layer for companies juggling multiple agent platforms, and said Agentforce and Slack have jointly delivered 7 billion "Agentic Work Units" so far, 3.2 billion of them in Q2 alone. It's a significant bet that enterprises want named, persistent digital employees with job titles rather than generic chatbots, timed deliberately for Salesforce's September 15–17 Dreamforce conference. Source: [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/salesforce-agentforce-job-ready-agents-dreamforce-2026/), [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/salesforce-agents-gain-a-runtime-that-pursues-goals-over-weeks-not-chats/)

**Sakana AI ships Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2, betting on orchestration over bigger models.** The Tokyo-based lab released two new models on September 11: Fugu Max and a flagship, Fugu Ultra v2.0, which is not a single foundation model but a learned "orchestrator" that routes tasks across a pool of other specialist models behind one OpenAI-compatible API, with a 1-million-token context window. Priced at $5/$30 per million input/output tokens (rising for contexts above 272K), it claims best-or-joint-best results on 5 of 8 benchmarks tested — including DeepSWE and a new tool-use benchmark called Toolathon — notably without including Anthropic's Fable 5.1 or OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra in its own routing pool. It's available worldwide via Sakana's API, OpenRouter and the Vercel AI Gateway, but not yet in the EU/EEA pending GDPR work. The release is a useful data point in a broader industry shift: rather than only chasing bigger single models, some labs are now competing on smarter routing across many specialized ones. Source: [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/10/sakana-ai-launches-fugu-max-and-fugu-ultra-v2-for-cheaper-stronger-multi-agent-orchestration/), [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/sakana-ai-ships-fugu-ultra-v2-to-route-tasks-across-specialist-ai-agents)

It's a small but telling data point alongside HiddenLayer's raise: a cluster of well-funded startups is now forming specifically around securing the AI agent stack, rather than the underlying models themselves. ## 3. VENTURE CAPITAL

**Global startup funding hit a record $510B in H1 2026 — and two companies took 43% of it.** Crunchbase's mid-year data, published in early July but still the most current comprehensive VC market snapshot available, showed global venture investment topping $510 billion in the first half of 2026, already surpassing all of 2025's $440 billion. The eye-opening detail: OpenAI and Anthropic alone accounted for $217 billion of that — 43% of all global startup funding in six months — an unprecedented concentration of venture capital into two companies. Exit activity also hit records, with 32 companies going public above $1 billion in Q2 and $113 billion in $1B+ acquisitions in the same quarter. It's the clearest quantitative picture yet of how thoroughly the frontier AI labs are distorting the entire venture asset class. Source: [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/)

**Former NBA player Omri Casspi closes Swish Ventures' third fund at $250M.** Reported September 3 by Forbes, Globes and Calcalist, the raise takes Casspi's five-year-old firm to roughly $800 million in AUM. Swish plans to deploy the new fund into about a dozen companies at an average check size of $20 million, co-leading or participating alongside other investors, with a focus on cybersecurity, infrastructure, physical AI, defense, industrial AI and govtech. The fund drew new institutional money, including from Sequoia Capital, on top of existing backers — notable given Swish's track record includes early bets on Cognition and Wonderful, among eight portfolio unicorns out of twenty investments to date. It's a good example of a still-small, sector-focused fund punching above its weight in a market dominated by mega-rounds. Source: [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/hanktucker/2026/09/03/how-nba-journeyman-omri-casspi-built-an-800-million-venture-capital-firm/), [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-omri-casspis-swish-ventures-closes-250m-fund-1001554339)

**PwC: AI infrastructure spending could hit $31.6 trillion through 2050 — reshaping where VC dollars flow.** A PwC report released September 2 projects that annual data-center capital expenditure will climb from roughly $800 billion a year today to $1.8 trillion a year by 2050. While not a VC deal itself, the forecast matters to the venture world because it's increasingly the thesis behind where growth-stage and infrastructure-focused funds are placing money — GPUs, power capacity, cooling and data-center buildout now absorb a disproportionate share of "AI" venture dollars (one tracker put AI Cluster Cloud deals at nearly half of all AI infrastructure financing, averaging roughly $800 million per round). It's the backdrop explaining why so much recent "startup" funding increasingly looks more like project finance for physical infrastructure than classic software venture capital. Source: [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/global-investment-in-ai-infrastructure.html)

## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY

### Dame Stephanie "Steve" Shirley: from Kindertransport refugee to Britain's software pioneer

In 1939, a five-year-old Jewish girl named Vera Buchthal boarded a train in Vienna, holding her nine-year-old sister's hand, and said goodbye to her mother. It was the Kindertransport — the rescue effort that brought roughly 10,000 unaccompanied children out of Nazi-controlled Europe to Britain in the wake of Kristallnacht. Vera never lived with her birth parents again. Raised by a foster family in England, she became a British citizen at 18 and renamed herself Stephanie Brook.

She built computers from scratch and wrote machine code at the Post Office Research Station through the 1950s, taking six years of night classes to earn a math degree while working full time — a path that was already unusual for a woman in British computing in that era. What followed was more so: after repeatedly hitting a ceiling of sexism, missed promotions and worse at every company she worked for, she decided in 1962 to start her own, founding Freelance Programmers from her dining room table with just £6 of capital.

The company — later FI Group, later still Xansa — was built almost entirely around women. Of its first 300 staff, only three were men; Shirley deliberately hired women, many of them mothers, to write software from home at a time when the very idea of "remote work" barely existed and the British tech industry offered women almost no path to serious technical careers. When she found that her business correspondence was routinely ignored under her own name, she started signing letters "Steve" instead — the response rate improved so dramatically that the nickname stuck for the rest of her life.

Over 31 years as chief executive, Shirley grew the company into a constituent of the FTSE 250, eventually selling it in 1993 for around £150 million — turning a number of her earliest employees, who held equity, into millionaires themselves. She was made a Dame for services to IT and later Companion of Honour. In retirement she gave away the bulk of her fortune, tens of millions of pounds, largely to autism research and services — driven by the experience of her son Giles, who had a severe form of autism and epilepsy and died at 35. She founded the Shirley Foundation in 1986, which helped establish Prior's Court School and the research charity Autistica.

Dame Stephanie died in August 2025 at 91. Her story is a reminder that some of the most consequential founders in tech history built their companies not despite exclusion, but as a direct, deliberate response to it.
