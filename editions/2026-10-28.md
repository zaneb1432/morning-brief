## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

*(This is the first edition in the repo, so there are no prior profiles to avoid repeating. Written from general knowledge, not fresh research.)*

Sarah Breedlove was born on a Louisiana cotton plantation in 1867, the first child in her family born free after emancipation. Orphaned at seven, married at fourteen and widowed at about twenty, she worked as a washerwoman in St. Louis for around $1.50 a day, raising her daughter A'Lelia.

In the 1890s she began losing her hair, which was common then because of poor nutrition, harsh products and infrequent washing. She worked for a time as a sales agent for Annie Malone's Poro hair-care company. In 1905 she moved to Denver and developed her own line of scalp-care products and treatments. She took the name "Madam C.J. Walker" after her marriage to Charles Joseph Walker, a newspaper advertising salesman who helped with early marketing.

Her business model was as innovative as the product. She sold door to door and by mail order, then built a network of trained independent "beauty culturists" selling her system, and later a school, Lelia College, to train them. Tens of thousands of Black women gained a path to income they controlled. She relocated her headquarters to Indianapolis, built a factory, and ran national conventions for her agents, which were among the first for a women's business organization. In Harlem she built a townhouse and promoted philanthropy: she funded scholarships, the NAACP's anti-lynching campaign and the YMCA.

She is often called the first female self-made millionaire in America, a claim historians debate because her exact net worth is uncertain, but her wealth and influence are not in doubt. She died in 1919 at age 51 from kidney failure and complications of hypertension. She left two-thirds of future profits to charity. Her daughter A'Lelia carried on the business and the Harlem social scene.

**Lesson for founders:** She built distribution first. Her sales network was the moat, and it doubled as a mission.
