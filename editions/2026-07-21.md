## TECH & AI NEWS

**1. Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 triggers a "DeepSeek moment 2.0," forcing a subscription pause as demand overwhelms capacity.**
Beijing-based Moonshot AI released Kimi K3 on July 17 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter, open-weight mixture-of-experts model with a 1-million-token context window, aimed at software engineering and agentic work, and priced at roughly $3/$15 per million input/output tokens. The model topped the Frontend Code Arena benchmark, and its cost-efficiency echoed the market shock of DeepSeek's 2025 release, sending Nvidia and other AI/semiconductor stocks lower and pushing the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index into a technical correction. Demand then surged roughly sixfold within days, and on July 19 Moonshot announced it was halting new Kimi K3 subscriptions because "our GPUs are feeling it," even as the startup pursues a Hong Kong IPO targeting a $30 billion-plus valuation. This is the clearest sign yet that Chinese open-weight labs are closing the frontier gap while undercutting US labs on price, rattling assumptions behind the AI infrastructure spending boom.
Source: [Eastern Herald](https://easternherald.com/2026/07/21/moonshot-ai-kimi-k3-subscriptions-paused-gpu-demand/)

**2. Alibaba previews Qwen3.8 Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model it claims ranks second only to Claude Fable 5.**
Unveiled July 19 at the World AI Conference in Shanghai — two days after Moonshot's Kimi K3 launch — Qwen3.8 Max is a sparse multimodal mixture-of-experts model handling text, images, video and documents with a 1-million-token context window. It's live now as "Qwen3.8-Max-Preview" on Alibaba's Token Plan, Qoder and QoderWork platforms at a promotional 10% of standard pricing, though Alibaba has released no independent benchmarks, model card, or license yet, and open weights remain only a "soon" promise. The near-simultaneous flagship launches from Moonshot and Alibaba underscore how quickly Chinese labs are now iterating at the frontier, adding pressure on US model providers' pricing just as global AI capex is projected to cross $1 trillion.
Source: [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/19/alibaba-previews-qwen3-8-max-a-2-4-trillion-parameter-multimodal-model-days-after-moonshots-kimi-k3-open-weight-launch/)

**3. China launches the World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO) as the 2026 World AI Conference closes in Shanghai.**
On July 16, twenty-nine countries — including Russia, Brazil, Belarus, Cuba, Venezuela, and a mix of African and Asian states — signed a founding agreement establishing WAICO, a Shanghai-headquartered intergovernmental body meant to coordinate global AI governance and expand AI access in the Global South. The signing preceded the four-day World AI Conference, where President Xi Jinping delivered his first-ever keynote on AI governance, and China pledged 5,000 AI training slots for developing countries plus new AI cooperation centers with ASEAN and the African Union. It's a significant geopolitical move, positioning China as a rival center of gravity for AI standard-setting — launching notably without the US, EU, UK, or Japan.
Source: [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-07-18/29-countries-join-World-AI-Cooperation-Organization-in-Shanghai-1OSeZzywfx6/p.html)

**4. EU orders Google to open Android and share search data with rival AI assistants under the Digital Markets Act.**
On July 16, the European Commission issued binding decisions requiring Google to give competing AI assistants deeper access to Android — including voice-activation parity with "Hey Google" and the ability to take in-app actions on a user's behalf — and to share anonymized search data with rival search engines and AI chatbots. Google must begin sharing search data by January 2027, with expanded AI-assistant access on Android rolling out from July 2027; Google's Kent Walker publicly objected, warning the rules could weaken user privacy and expose business secrets. It's one of the most consequential DMA enforcement actions yet against Google's AI strategy, aimed at preventing Gemini from entrenching itself via default OS-level advantages.
Source: [European Commission — Digital Markets Act](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/commission-provides-guidance-google-ai-interoperability-android-and-sharing-google-search-data-under-2026-07-16_en)

**5. Google DeepMind's Gemini 3.5 Pro remains unshipped after a third delay, still MIA as of July 18.**
Originally targeted for June 2026, then July, then a widely reported July 17 date, Gemini 3.5 Pro has slipped again with no public launch date, pricing, model card, or benchmarks disclosed. Reporting indicates DeepMind scrapped the existing Gemini 2.5 Pro architecture for a ground-up rebuild after the model fell short of internal quality goals — particularly on coding performance and hallucination/reliability metrics. The delay cedes ground to OpenAI's GPT-5.6 (released July 9) and Anthropic's offerings just as Chinese labs like Moonshot and Alibaba ship frontier-class models at a rapid clip, intensifying pressure on Google to ship a credible answer.
Source: [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/gemini/articles/where-gemini-3-5-pro-200026497.html)

## STARTUP WORLD

**1. Netflix pays $587 million for Ben Affleck's AI filmmaking startup InterPositive.**
Netflix disclosed in a Form 10-Q filing that it paid $587 million in cash to acquire InterPositive, an AI filmmaking startup that actor-director Ben Affleck co-founded in 2022 and ran in stealth mode. The company builds tools that let filmmakers train AI models on their own production dailies to assist with post-production work such as color mixing, relighting shots, and visual effects — explicitly designed to keep filmmakers "at the center of the process" rather than replace them. The deal, completed earlier this year but only revealed this week, brings InterPositive's 16-person team into Netflix, with Affleck joining as a senior advisor. It's one of the largest Hollywood-AI startup acquisitions to date, and signals how seriously major studios are investing in proprietary, filmmaker-controlled generative tools rather than relying on third-party AI vendors.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/19/netflix-paid-587m-for-ben-afflecks-ai-filmmaking-startup/)

**2. CuspAI raises $450 million Series B at a $2.6 billion valuation to launch an "AI Materials Foundry."**
Cambridge/Amsterdam-based CuspAI, which uses AI to discover new physical materials, announced a $450 million Series B co-led by Kleiner Perkins, NEA, and Jeff Bezos' Bezos Expeditions, pushing its valuation to $2.6 billion just nine months after a prior raise near $520 million. The round included Lux Capital, AMD Ventures, John Doerr, Temasek, Prosus, and sovereign funds from the UK and Netherlands, alongside the launch of an "AI Materials Foundry" — a network of more than 45 founding partners including Nvidia, Meta, Samsung, and Applied Materials. Founded in 2024 by chemist Chad Edwards and machine-learning researcher Max Welling, CuspAI aims to compress years-long materials-discovery cycles (batteries, semiconductors, catalysts) into months using generative models. It's a striking example of frontier AI investment spilling from software into physical-science and hard-tech domains.
Source: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/20/ai-materials-science-startup-cuspai-raises-450m-funding/)

**3. Neo emerges from stealth with $100 million to secure enterprise AI agents.**
Israeli startup Neo launched publicly with $100 million in combined funding — a $75 million Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz and Bessemer Venture Partners, on top of a previously undisclosed $25 million seed round from 2025. Founded by former SentinelOne executives Nick Warner (who led SentinelOne's go-to-market org through its 2021 IPO), Shlomi Salem, and Eran Shirazi, the company builds security tooling for a new risk category: autonomous AI agents operating inside browsers, developer tools, and SaaS apps using legitimate employee credentials. It reflects a fast-growing wedge of cybersecurity VC dollars — "agentic AI security" — as enterprises grapple with AI systems that act with real permissions but without the guardrails built for human users.
Source: [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/neo-emerges-from-stealth-with-100m-to-control-and-secure-enterprise-ai-software/)

**4. AI inference startup Infinity raises $15 million seed at a $100 million valuation.**
Infinity, founded by former Google Brain researcher Nixon (also a co-founder of the AGI House hacker network), raised a $15 million seed round from Touring Capital, Principal VC, and individual researchers from OpenAI and Anthropic. Its core product, an autonomous coding agent called Ignition, automatically writes and optimizes low-level compute kernels for new AI chips — tackling one of the industry's biggest bottlenecks outside Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem. Infinity says the platform already has commercial partnerships, including with chipmaker d-Matrix. The round reflects growing investor appetite for picks-and-shovels AI infrastructure that reduces dependence on Nvidia, at a moment when chip diversification is a top strategic priority.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/inference-startup-infinity-raises-15m-from-touring-capital-openai-and-athropic-researchers/)

**5. Nonprofit Current AI launches Suno Sutra, an offline AI device for 22 Indian languages.**
Current AI — a nonprofit founded in 2025 by Martin Tisne with $400 million in commitments from the French government, the Ford and MacArthur Foundations, DeepMind, and Salesforce — unveiled Suno Sutra ("listening chronicles" in Hindi), a pocket-sized, fully offline device that runs AI models locally across 22 Indian languages with no internet connection required. Built with Bhashini, the Indian government's AI language division, the open-sourced device is meant to let developer communities build language tools for populations underserved by mainstream, internet-dependent, English-centric AI systems. It's a notable example of nonprofit-backed, public-interest AI infrastructure emerging as a counterweight to venture-funded frontier labs.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/19/nonprofit-current-ai-is-racing-to-build-the-world-wide-web-of-ai-free-for-all/)

## VENTURE CAPITAL

**1. Greylock caps its 18th fund at $1.5 billion to back AI-native founders.**
Greylock Partners, one of Silicon Valley's oldest venture firms, announced it had closed Greylock 18 at $1.5 billion — 50% larger than its prior 2023 vehicle — to back founders at the earliest stages of AI-native companies across infrastructure, cybersecurity, applications, deep tech, consumer, and fintech. Notably, partner Saam Motamedi told TechCrunch the firm could have raised a multiple of that amount but deliberately capped it, betting that discipline (roughly one or two new investments per partner per year, yielding ~25 portfolio companies) beats chasing mega-fund scale in a market flooded with capital. It's a pointed counter-signal to the AI-driven fund-size inflation seen elsewhere in the industry this year, including a16z's own $15B-plus raises.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/why-greylock-capped-its-new-fund-at-1-5b-when-it-says-it-could-have-raised-more/)

**2. CuspAI's $450 million Series B: venture capital rotating into physical-world AI R&D.**
CuspAI's round (also noted above) is worth flagging from the investor side: Bezos Expeditions co-leading alongside Kleiner Perkins and NEA, with existing customers already including ASML, Meta, Samsung, and Hyundai, is a striking example of venture dollars moving beyond foundation models and into AI applied to hard, physical-world problems — semiconductors, batteries, and industrial materials — rather than another chat interface or coding tool.
Source: [TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/cuspai-400m-series-b-2-6b-valuation-bezos-materials-search/)

**3. Natural raises $30 million to build payments infrastructure for AI agents.**
San Francisco-based Natural announced a $30 million Series A led by Forerunner's Kirsten Green, bringing the roughly 193-day-old startup's total funding above $40 million. Natural is building an "agent orchestration" payments layer so AI agents can autonomously send, collect, and hold funds — something traditional rails like credit cards and ACH can't support because they assume a human authorizes every transaction. It's a concrete data point for a thesis gaining real traction among VCs this summer: as agentic AI moves from chat interfaces to actually transacting, financial infrastructure built for human-initiated payments needs to be rebuilt for machine-initiated ones.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/natural-raises-30m-to-reinvent-payments-for-ai-agents-and-take-on-stripe/)

**4. Empirical Security raises $25 million Series A for AI-driven exposure management.**
Chicago-based Empirical Security announced a $25 million Series A led by Brightmind Partners with continued backing from HPA and Costanoa Ventures, lifting total funding to $37 million. Founded by Ed Bellis and Michael Roytman (previously of Kenna Security), the company builds predictive models — Foundation, which tracks over 18,000 exploited CVEs globally, and Radiant, a customer-specific predictive engine — to forecast which vulnerabilities will actually be exploited rather than just flagging every possible exposure. The round reflects a broader VC shift away from generic vulnerability scanning and toward AI systems that predict and prioritize real-world attacker behavior.
Source: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/enterprise-software-deals/2026/07/20/cybersecurity-empirical-security-ai-25-million)

## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY: Momofuku Ando

In August 1958, in a ramshackle wooden shed behind his house in Ikeda, a suburb of Osaka, a 48-year-old man who had just lost everything sat down to a bowl of noodles he'd spent a year perfecting. He had no company, no fortune, and a stretch in prison behind him. What he had was a hunch: that the humble noodle, not bread, could feed a starving postwar Japan — and that if he could just figure out how to dry it so it would keep and cook in minutes, he could build something out of the wreckage of his life. That noodle became Chicken Ramen, and the man, Momofuku Ando, would go on to found Nissin and invent an entire category of food eaten today by people in nearly every country on earth.

Ando's path there was anything but a straight line. Born Go Pek-Hok in 1910 to a Taiwanese family in Japanese-controlled Chiayi, he was orphaned young and raised by grandparents who ran a small textile shop — his first exposure to commerce. By 22 he'd started his own textile company in Taipei; by 23 he'd moved to Osaka and built a clothing business while studying economics at night. Then came the war, which gutted his company, and the chaotic postwar years, during which he cycled through ventures in salt trading and founding a night school. In 1948 he was charged with tax evasion — his offense, by some accounts, was funding scholarships for students in a way regulators deemed improper — and spent nearly two years in prison. He emerged in his late thirties with his business bankrupt and his reputation in tatters.

Rather than walk away from commerce, Ando fixated on a problem he'd noticed while watching hungry crowds line up at black-market noodle stalls: Japan's government was pushing American-donated wheat into bread, even though noodles were what people actually craved, because no noodle manufacturer had the scale to supply the whole country reliably. Ando decided he would build that scale himself, from nothing. Working alone in his backyard shed, sleeping around four hours a night for a year, he tried to solve the puzzle of a noodle that was already cooked, could be stored without refrigeration, and only needed hot water. The breakthrough came, as the story is told, while watching his wife Masako fry tempura: the scalding oil flash-dried the batter, punching microscopic holes through it. He applied the same flash-frying trick to noodles, and the holes that resulted let boiling water rehydrate them in minutes. Chicken Ramen launched in 1958 at a price roughly six times that of fresh noodles — and sold out anyway, as "magic ramen" nobody could quite believe.

Ando didn't stop there. On a 1966 sales trip to the U.S., he watched a supermarket buyer with no bowl on hand break a block of noodles into a paper coffee cup, pour in hot water, and eat it with a fork. That improvised meal became Cup Noodles, launched in 1971 after five more years of engineering — a product built explicitly to fit how other cultures actually ate, not how Japan did. Ando ran Nissin until his death in 2007 at 96, repeating one line for decades: "Peace will come to the world when the people have enough to eat." It's a founder's philosophy forged in prison and a backyard shed, by a man who treated a second act, started at an age most people call it quits, as the only act that mattered.
