## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Soichiro Honda (1906–1991)

*No prior editions exist in this repo, so there is nothing to avoid repeating. Written from general knowledge, not fresh research.*

Soichiro Honda was born in 1906 in a village near Hamamatsu, Japan, the son of a blacksmith and bicycle repairer. He left school at about 15 to apprentice at a Tokyo garage, Art Shokai, where he learned to repair cars. He later opened a branch of the shop in Hamamatsu and began racing. After a serious crash, he turned to engineering.

His first business was piston rings. He sold them to Toyota, only to have the first batch rejected for poor quality. Rather than quit, he went back to school to study metallurgy, which was ridiculed at the time, and eventually produced rings that passed. Wartime bombing and an earthquake destroyed his factories, and after the war he sold what was left of his operation to Toyota.

In 1946 he started the Honda Technical Research Institute, first attaching small surplus generator engines to bicycles to help people get around in a fuel-starved Japan. Honda Motor Co. was founded in 1948. The 1949 Dream motorcycle and then the 1958 Super Cub made the company, and the Super Cub became one of the best-selling motor vehicles ever built. He entered racing, the Isle of Man TT in 1959, and famously treated racing as an engineering laboratory.

Partnership mattered. Takeo Fujisawa handled finance and sales while Honda handled engineering, and Honda credited him with the company's survival. In 1973, Honda stepped down as president rather than stay too long, and he spent his later years supporting engineering and community causes.

**Lessons:** Treat failure as data (the rejected piston rings). Pair a product obsessive with a business operator. Use the hard problem in front of you, in his case a mobility need after the war, as the opening for a company.
