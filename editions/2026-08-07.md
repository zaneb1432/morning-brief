## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY: Isaiah Taylor

Today's biggest funding story has an unusually young name behind it. Isaiah Taylor, founder and CEO of Valar Atomics — the nuclear-reactor startup that just raised $1 billion from Sequoia at a $6 billion valuation — dropped out of high school at 16. He'd already taught himself to code and built up a body of freelance and side-project work, which was enough to land him a six-figure coding job without a diploma.

Coding wasn't the destination, though — it was capital and credibility for something harder. Taylor had been fixated on nuclear physics for roughly a decade, and he'd concluded that if he was eventually going to attempt something as difficult as building nuclear reactors, he needed operating experience first. So before Valar, he started an auto repair shop — an unglamorous, hands-on detour that taught him how to actually run a company, manage supply chains, and ship physical things on a deadline, lessons a physics obsession alone wouldn't have given him.

He founded Valar Atomics in 2023 on a specific, contrarian thesis: nuclear energy's problem was never really physics. It was that every plant in the industry's history has essentially been built as a bespoke, one-off megaproject, with all the cost overruns and delays that implies. Valar's answer is standardized, mass-manufactured, factory-built reactors — treating nuclear power plants more like an industrial product than a civil-engineering event.

That bet looked increasingly prescient as AI data centers turned electricity into the industry's binding constraint. In June, Valar's "Ward 250" — a 30MW waterless microreactor — became the first startup-built advanced reactor to achieve self-sustaining criticality and directly power an Nvidia Blackwell AI cluster, leading to an official collaboration with Nvidia on fully atomic-powered data centers. That milestone is what pulled Sequoia in: partner Shaun Maguire led this week's $1 billion round, a nearly eightfold jump from Valar's $130 million raise less than a year earlier, on the theory that solving the AI industry's power bottleneck is now one of the biggest infrastructure opportunities in venture. Three years after founding a company in one of the most heavily regulated, capital-intensive industries in the world — as a college-less 24-year-old — Taylor has turned a personal physics obsession into one of the defining infrastructure bets of the AI buildout.
