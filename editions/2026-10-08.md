## FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY: Masaru Ibuka (1908–1997), the engineer behind Sony

Born April 11, 1908, in Nikkō, Japan, Ibuka lost his father, an electrochemistry technologist, in an electrocution accident and was raised largely by his grandparents, who fed his love of technology. He studied electrical engineering at Waseda University (1933) and was nicknamed a "genius inventor" as a student. He worked on sound and picture recording and vacuum tubes, then wartime equipment such as submarine detection instruments.

In 1945, amid the ruins of Tokyo, he founded a small research lab, Tokyo Tsushin Kenkyusho. In 1946 it became a company with Akio Morita and Kazuo Maeda. Their first big product was Japan's first tape recorder. Ibuka became president in 1950 and in 1954 made Sony the first Asian licensee of Western Electric's transistor patent. His team later developed the Trinitron color TV (1967). The division of labor was classic: Ibuka was the engineer, Morita the marketer. The company took the name SONY in 1958. Ibuka was president until 1971, then chairman until retiring in 1976, and received the IEEE Founders Medal in 1972. He died December 19, 1997, aged 89.

Lesson: build the technical team first, and let a partner carry the story to the world. Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaru_Ibuka) · [ETHW](https://ethw.org/Masaru_Ibuka) · [Commoncog](https://commoncog.com/c/cases/birth-of-sony/)
