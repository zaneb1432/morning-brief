## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Madam C.J. Walker (1867–1919)

Sarah Breedlove was born on a Louisiana cotton plantation to formerly enslaved parents, the first of her family born free. Orphaned at seven, married at 14, and widowed by 20 with a young daughter, A'Lelia, she worked as a washerwoman in St. Louis for roughly $1.50 a day. In the 1890s she began losing her hair to scalp disease, a common affliction in an era of poor sanitation and harsh products.

Her pivot came after working as a sales agent for Annie Malone's Poro hair-care company and, by her account, a dream that revealed a restorative formula. In 1906 she married newspaperman Charles Joseph Walker, whose promotion savvy gave her the brand: **Madam C.J. Walker**. She sold door to door, demonstrated in churches and homes, and built a mail-order business out of Denver and then Pittsburgh and Indianapolis.

Her real innovation was distribution. She trained thousands of Black women as "beauty culturists," giving them independence at a time when domestic work was often their only option. She ran Lelia College to train agents, held conventions to recognize top sellers, and organized agents into the National Beauty Culturists and Benevolent Association. By the 1910s she employed tens of thousands of agents, built a factory in Indianapolis, and became widely described as the first self-made female millionaire in America (a claim historians debate but which her estate supports).

She funded the NAACP's anti-lynching campaign, gave to the YMCA and to Black colleges, and built a Hudson River estate, Villa Lewaro. She died in 1919 at 51 of kidney failure and hypertension. The lesson: she built a brand, a training pipeline and a community with a network of sellers decades before "platform" was a word.
