## 4. FOUNDER BIOGRAPHY — Ingvar Kamprad (1926–2018), IKEA

Ingvar Kamprad grew up on Elmtaryd, a farm near the village of Agunnaryd in Småland, a poor, rocky region of southern Sweden known for thrifty people. He started selling before he was a teenager. As a boy he bought matches in bulk in Stockholm and resold them one by one to neighbors at a small profit. He went on to sell fish he caught, Christmas cards, seeds and pencils, delivering them by bicycle. In 1943, at 17, he used a small cash gift from his father for doing well in school to register a company. He named it **IKEA** from his initials (I.K.), the farm (Elmtaryd) and the parish (Agunnaryd). He worked out of a kitchen table at his uncle's house.

At first IKEA was a mail-order business for small goods: pens, wallets, picture frames, watches and nylon stockings, whatever Kamprad could buy cheaply and sell for less than local shops. In 1948 he added furniture made by small workshops around Småland, and in 1951 he published the first IKEA catalogue. Mail order ran into a quality problem, because competitors cut prices by cutting corners and customers complained. Kamprad's answer was to open a showroom in **Älmhult** in 1953 so customers could see and touch the furniture before they bought it. That combination of low prices and products customers could inspect themselves became the basis of the IKEA store.

The industry fought back. Swedish furniture manufacturers, pressured by established retailers, boycotted IKEA in the 1950s and barred it from trade fairs. Kamprad responded by doing more of the work himself. IKEA started designing its own furniture and, in 1961, began sourcing from manufacturers in Communist Poland, which was unusual and controversial during the Cold War. Around the same time came the **flat pack**. According to company lore, designer Gillis Lundgren took the legs off a table so it would fit in a customer's car, and the idea grew into knock-down furniture that customers carry home and assemble themselves. Shipping volume dropped sharply, and customers in effect did the delivery and assembly. The first full-scale store opened in Älmhult in 1958, and a large round flagship opened outside Stockholm in 1965, with a self-serve warehouse added after customer crowds overwhelmed the staff.

Kamprad laid out his philosophy in *The Testament of a Furniture Dealer* (1976), a short manifesto about cost-consciousness, simplicity and "doing more with less." He lived by it in public. He drove an old Volvo for decades, flew economy and was known to take salt and pepper packets from restaurants. He also designed the company's structure with care. To avoid Swedish taxes, he moved to Denmark in 1973 and then to Switzerland in 1976. In 1982 he moved ownership of the main IKEA group into a Dutch foundation, **Stichting INGKA Foundation**, and the trademark into a separate entity, Inter IKEA. The arrangement made the company very hard to take over or break up, and very tax-efficient, and critics have studied it for years.

His legacy has dark chapters. In 1994 Swedish journalists revealed that as a teenager in the 1940s he had been involved with a Swedish pro-Nazi movement. Kamprad wrote to IKEA employees calling it "the greatest mistake of my life" and apologized. A 2011 book by Elisabeth Åsbrink showed the involvement had been deeper and lasted longer than he had admitted. He also spoke openly about years of struggling with alcohol, and he had dyslexia. One popular account says IKEA gives products Swedish names instead of number codes because he found the numbers hard to remember. He gradually stepped back from operations, left the Inter IKEA board in 2013, moved back to Småland, and died there in January 2018 at 91.

**Lessons for founders:** Kamprad's biggest breakthroughs came from constraints. The boycott pushed IKEA into designing its own furniture and finding new suppliers. A table that didn't fit in a car led to the flat pack. Thin margins pushed him to have customers do part of the work, and he turned that into something they valued. He built a cost advantage into how the company operated and wrote that discipline down so it would outlast him.
